DEFINITIONS
D1 [method] MSARIMA analytic interval: The quantiles of `predict(interval="prediction")` of a pure ARIMA in smooth 1.3.0, which dispatches to the approximate interval. The ARIMA is the state space model $y_t = w^\top v_{t-l} + \epsilon_t$, $v_t = F v_{t-l} + g\epsilon_t$, $\epsilon_t \sim N(0, \sigma^2)$. The quantile at level $\tau$ and horizon $h$ is $q_{\tau,h} = \hat y_{T+h|T} + \Phi^{-1}(\tau)\sqrt{V_h}$ with $V_h = \hat\sigma^2 \frac{T}{T-k} c_h$, where $\hat\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} e_t^2$, $k$ the number of estimated parameters and $c_h$ the $h$-th diagonal element of the analytic covariance of the state space model, $c_h = 1 + \sum_{j=1}^{h-1} (w^\top F^{j-1} g)^2$ for a model with lag 1. [source B6]
D2 [data] known-model simulation: Gaussian ARIMA series with $\sigma = 10$ generated from 11 data generating processes (white noise with a constant, random walk with and without drift, AR(1), MA(1), ARIMA(0,1,1), ARIMA(1,1,0), ARIMA(1,1,1), ARIMA(0,2,2), seasonal SARIMA(0,1,1)(0,1,1)$_{12}$ and SARIMA(1,0,0)(1,0,0)$_{12}$) plus an IMA(1,1) with Student-t errors on 4 degrees of freedom; in-sample lengths $T$ of 15, 25, 50, 100 and 300, holdout of 18; 2,000 replicates per process and length with seed 20261105 unless stated.
D3 [design] cases T, E, S, W: What the fitted AutoMSARIMA or MSARIMA knows about the data generating process of the [[D2:known-model simulation]]: T, true orders and true AR, MA and constant parameters with initial states and scale estimated; E, true orders with estimated parameters; S, orders selected by AutoMSARIMA; W, a fixed wrong model for each process. Each with backcasting or optimal initial states.
D4 [variable] coverage: For a quantile $q_\tau$, the share of holdout observations at or below it, $c_\tau = \frac{1}{h}\sum_{j=1}^{h} 1(y_{T+j} \le q_{\tau,j})$, averaged first within a series and then over series. cov80 is $c_{0.9} - c_{0.1}$, the coverage of the central 80% interval; q50 is $c_{0.5}$.
D5 [data] benchmark: The 5,315 series of M1 (1,001), M3 (3,003) and Tourism (1,311) from fcompdata 0.1.4, with their own holdouts and seasonal periods; lags $(1, m)$ for seasonal series. [members D18, D19, D20]
D6 [data] M4 sample: 1,500 series each of the yearly, quarterly and monthly M4 series, drawn with seed 20261105 without replacement, with the M4 holdouts. [source B4]
D7 [design] level terciles: Series split into terciles of the [[D17:level ratio]] across the series of a data set; cov80 reported for each tercile, and the spread as the largest minus the smallest of the three.
D8 [method] auto.adam error selection: R `auto.adam(y, model="NNN", lags, distribution=c("dnorm","dlnorm"), initial="backcasting")` of smooth 4.6: the ARIMA orders and constant are selected by AICc for each distribution with the maxima of auto.msarima (AR (3,3), I (2,1), MA (3,3)), then the distribution with the lower AICc is kept; with dnorm the model is the [[D10:AutoMSARIMA]] model, with dlnorm it is the [[D11:log-normal ARIMA]]. Quantiles from `forecast(interval="prediction")`. [source B6]
D9 [data] fresh M4 sample: 1,500 series each of the yearly, quarterly and monthly M4 series, drawn with seed 20261107 from the series not in the [[D6:M4 sample]], so disjoint from it. [source B4]
D10 [method] AutoMSARIMA: `AutoMSARIMA(lags, initial)` in Python and `auto.msarima()` in R of smooth 1.3.0 / 4.6: ARIMA orders up to AR (3,3), I (2,1), MA (3,3) and a constant selected by AICc, normal errors, the [[D1:MSARIMA analytic interval]] for the quantiles. Backcasting initial states unless stated. [source B6]
D11 [method] log-normal ARIMA: ADAM with `model="NNN"`, an ARIMA and `distribution="dlnorm"`: the ARIMA applies to the logarithms, $\log y_t = w^\top v_{t-l} + \log(1+\epsilon_t)$, $v_t = F v_{t-l} + g \log(1+\epsilon_t)$, $1+\epsilon_t \sim \mathrm{logN}(-\sigma^2/2, \sigma^2)$, so the scale of the error is proportional to the level. In the comparisons with fixed orders, the orders and constant are those [[D10:AutoMSARIMA]] selects on the same series. [source B6]
D12 [method] reforecast: `reforecast()` of smooth: draws the parameters 300 times from a normal distribution with the estimated covariance of the parameters, reapplies the model with each draw and simulates the predictive distribution, so that the quantiles include the uncertainty of the parameters.
D13 [variable] MCE: Mean calibration error over the 99 quantile levels $\tau = 0.01, \dots, 0.99$: $\frac{1}{99}\sum_\tau |\bar c_\tau - \tau|$, with $\bar c_\tau$ the [[D4:coverage]] of $q_\tau$.
D14 [variable] scaled pinball: Pinball loss averaged over the 99 quantile levels and the horizons, divided by the mean absolute first difference of the in-sample data for real series; in the simulation, by $\sigma$, and relpin is its ratio to the pinball of the true predictive distribution.
D15 [method] ETS forced and selected: ETS of smooth with backcasting: AutoADAM-style selection among ETS models (`ES(model="ZXZ")`, or ZXN for non-seasonal series), or ETS(ANN) or ETS(MNN) fitted to every series. [source B6]
D16 [method] other ARIMA implementations: statsforecast AutoARIMA, pmdarima auto_arima and skforecast Arima, as run in the notebook of the smooth benchmark of 2026-11-05, whose saved forecasts were read.
D17 [variable] level ratio: Mean of the last third of the in-sample data of a series divided by the mean of all its in-sample data.
D18 [data] M1: The 1,001 series of the M1 competition with their holdouts. [source B1]
D19 [data] M3: The 3,003 series of the M3 competition with their holdouts. [source B2]
D20 [data] Tourism: The 1,311 series of the tourism forecasting competition with their holdouts. [source B3]
D21 [setting] smooth 1.0.8 and 1.3.0: Python smooth at tag v1.0.8-py (a separate worktree with its own environment) and at 1.3.0 (commit 634657d8 of openforecast-org/smooth).

WARRANTS
W1 [abductive; premises C10, C11] Where ETS and ARIMA reduce to the same model, their intervals coincide and cover the same share (C17), so the interval computations agree; the additive ETS(ANN) forced on every series covers no more than ARIMA(0,1,1), while the multiplicative ETS(MNN) covers 0.07 to 0.10 more (C22). The difference between the pools is therefore the multiplicative-error models, which AutoMSARIMA does not have.

LIMITATIONS
L1 [sampling; affects C16; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation none] The 12 wrong models of case W were chosen by the authors, not drawn from the mistakes AutoMSARIMA makes, so the size of the coverage loss they produce depends on that choice.
L2 [other; affects C2, C3, C4; reduces_precision; mitigation none] The lab record flags plans PL11, PL12 and PL13 with code changed after the plan; the only commit between those plans and their runs updated the claimset skills under .claude/skills, not the analysis scripts.
L3 [measurement; affects C2; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] ETS(MNN), the comparator whose coverage stays flat over the level terciles on M4, is badly centred there (q50 of 0.372) and has an MCE of 0.0849, so its flat cov80 does not make it well calibrated.
L4 [computational; affects C4; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation none] smooth 1.0.8 ran from a separate worktree at tag v1.0.8-py with its own Python environment, so the versions also differ in the environment around the package.
L5 [computational; affects C5, C20; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The auto.adam comparison ran in R, because Python's AutoADAM removes dlnorm from the candidate distributions of a pure ARIMA whatever the user passes, while R's auto.adam removes it only from its default list.
L6 [analytic_flexibility; affects C5; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation none] The thresholds of plan PL15 were fixed after the exploratory benchmark run of auto.adam (RN45) was seen, on M4 series that run did not use.
L7 [sample_size; affects C10; reduces_precision; mitigation none] The series where the selected ETS(ANN) and ARIMA(0,1,1) give the same forecast and width are 114 of 5,315.
L8 [model_specification; affects C21; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation none] The prediction of coverage at the benchmark lengths uses Gaussian processes and interpolates the simulated coverage over log length, with lengths outside 15 to 300 at the nearest simulated size.

OPEN QUESTIONS
O1 [from C5, C22] Why do the quantiles of auto.adam with error selection lie too high, by about 0.08 to 0.13 of coverage between the 0.2 and 0.6 quantiles, on the series of the highest level tercile, and would a model of the location of grown series correct it?
O2 [from C15, C17] Why do the parameter draws of reforecast() give explosive or several times too wide intervals for ARIMA models with a constant or AR terms, differently in R and Python?
O3 [from C3, C19, C20] Would AutoMSARIMA calibrate better with error selection than with a fixed log-normal error once the location error of grown series is addressed, given that the fixed log-normal error costs 11% of median pinball on the benchmark?
O4 [from C4, C23] Which of the changes between smooth 1.0.8 and 1.3.0 that alter model selection (fewer drift models chosen with backcasting) makes the benchmark calibration of AutoMSARIMA worse while its calibration on known models improves?
