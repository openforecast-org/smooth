# smooth (Python) NEWS

Release history of the Python implementation of the **smooth** forecasting package.


## v1.2.0 (unreleased)

Changes:
* `predict()` and `reforecast()` of `ADAM` (and the combinations) and of `TBATS` take `point="skeleton" | "mean" | "median"`, as R's `point`: the `"skeleton"`, the default, is the forecast returned so far (the model run forward with the future errors at their neutral values); `"mean"` is the conditional mean, the skeleton where they coincide and the mean of `nsim` simulated paths otherwise; `"median"` is the 50% quantile of the method of the interval. The mean and median are computed in the shared `forecaster()`. For `TBATS` the skeleton is the median, and the mean is computed by Gauss-Hermite quadrature with `dnorm` (as R, to 1e-10) and from simulated paths otherwise. `reforecast()` returned the trimmed mean of its paths: it is now the skeleton by default, as R. `seed` now also makes the simulated intervals and the simulated point forecasts reproducible; it was used by `reforecast()` only.
* `TBATS`, a port of R's new `tbats()`: De Livera et al.'s model in ADAM's Single Source of Error form (ETS level and trend, trigonometric seasonality with fractional periods, ADAM's ARMA, all in the Box-Cox space), with the harmonics, the trend and the ARMA orders selected by the information criterion, `predict()` through ADAM's forecaster in the transformed space (and through `reforecast()` for the `"confidence"` and `"complete"` intervals), `reapply()`, `simulate()`, `point_lik()`, `vcov()`, `confint()` and `coefbootstrap()`. Explanatory variables enter through `fit(y, X)` in the transformed space, with `regressors="use"`, `"select"` (greybox's `stepwise()` on the errors of the model without them, as ADAM in R) or `"adapt"` (`delta1`, ...); `predict(h, X)` takes their future values, else the holdout, else their ADAM forecasts with a warning. On the same data it returns R's structure, parameters, likelihood, forecasts, covariance and confidence intervals, with and without regressors (`tests/test_tbats_r_parity.py`).
* The fitted attributes of `TBATS` take the names of `ADAM`'s: `orders` (with the `i` orders, as R's `$orders`), `n_param` (the table of R's `$nParam`), `fisher_information_`, `profile`, `initial_type` and `time_elapsed`.
* `CES` takes the names of `ADAM`: `initial_value` (was `initial_states_`), `initial_type` (`initial_type_`), `scale` (`scale_`), `transition` and `measurement` (`transition_matrix`, `measurement_matrix`), `nparam` for the number of parameters and `n_param` for R's `$nParam` table (`n_param` was the number), `coef` and `b_value` for B (`B`), and `states` with the components in rows and without the extra head of backcasting, as `ADAM`'s. `forecast_` is gone: the forecasts are `predict().mean`. It gains `coef_names` (R's names of B), `nobs`, `loss_`, `distribution_`, `error_type`, `sigma`, `profile`, `time_elapsed`, `actuals`, `data`, `holdout_data`, `lags_used`, and `fisher_information_` with `fi=True` (and `step_size`), all as R's. The settings of its two optimisers go in `nlopt_kwargs`, as `ADAM`'s, with the keys of R's ellipsis of `ces()` (`algorithm0`, `maxeval0`, `xtol_rel0`, ... for the first one); they were separate arguments.
* The states of `CES` and the initials in `B` take R's names, which follow `ADAM`'s: `level`, `potential`, `seasonal`, `seasonalPotential` (`seasonal1`, `seasonalPotential1`, ... with several lags; they were `seasonal 1`, `seasonal[4]`, `level.s`, ...), and `seasonal_1`, `seasonalPotential_1`, `seasonal1_1`, ... for the initials.
* `CES(initial="two-stage")` with `seasonality="full"` or `"simple"` started from the seasonal initials of the backcast fit in the wrong order (component by component instead of time by time), and ended in another optimum than R (log-likelihood -541.06 against R's -540.91 on AirPassengers, -618.49 against -614.18 for the simple CES). The names of the initial seasonal states in `coef_names` are R's.
* `CES` has the methods of `ADAM`, as R's `ces()` returns an `adam` object: `vcov()` (the OPG covariance as R's `covarOPGces()`, or the observed Fisher Information), `confint()`, `rstandard()`, `rstudent()`, `outlierdummy()`, `extract_scale()`, `extract_sigma()`, `plot()`, `rmultistep()`, `multicov()`, `simulate()` (from R's initial profile), `predict()` (with the `"confidence"` and `"complete"` intervals, `point`, `seed`), `reapply()` and `reforecast()` (with R's CES branches: the eigenvalue bounds and the transition and persistence of each draw) and `coefbootstrap()` (refitting `CES`), with R's results. `nlopt_kwargs` takes `B`, `lb` and `ub`, as `ADAM`'s and R's ellipsis of `ces()`. The scale sums the squared errors with R's accumulator. `reapply()` of `ADAM` starts the refits from the profile the fit started from, R's `$profileInitial`, where a model keeps it apart from its states.
* `AutoCES.fit()` returns the selected `CES`, as R's `auto.ces()` and `AutoOM`, with the information criteria of the candidates in `ICs` and the time of the selection in `time_elapsed`; it returned the `AutoCES` with `best_model_` and `models_`.
* `ADAM.persistence_vector` is R's `$persistence` as a dict under R's names (`alpha`, `beta`, `gamma` or `gamma1`, ..., `psi` or `psi1`, ... for ARIMA, `delta1`, ... for the regressors), as its docstring said; it was the bare column of the persistence vector. `persistence_level_`, `persistence_trend_`, `persistence_seasonal_` and `persistence_xreg_` are the estimated values, read from it: they held the provided values only (None when estimated). `OM` gets them from `ADAM`, so they are None rather than missing when the model has no such component.
* `SMA` stores its information criteria in `ICs`, as the other models (was `ICs_`).
* `ADAM` (and the classes built on it) has `ICs`, R's `$ICs`: the information criteria of the models in the pool, by name, with a selection or a combination, and of the model otherwise. The docstring promised them as `ic_selection`.
* `TBATS` takes `outliers="ignore" | "use" | "select"` and `outliers_level`, as `ADAM` and R's `tbats()`: the outliers are found on the residuals of the global model (no fit is added), and their dummies join the regressors of all the fits, with zeros over the horizon; `"select"` chooses among them and their leads and lags with `stepwise()`. `regressors` becomes `"use"` or `"select"`, as in R's `auto.adam()`.
* The dummies of the outliers of `ADAM` and `TBATS` are zero over the horizon, as in R: `predict()` and `reforecast()` do not forecast them without `X` (no warning when they are the only regressors), and add them as zeros to an `X` with the other regressors only.
* With `outliers="use"` or `"select"`, the regressors of `ADAM` and `TBATS` named as the dummies of the outliers (`outlier1`, `outlier1Lag1`, ...) are renamed to `x.outlier1`, ..., with a warning, as in R.
* `TBATS.sm()` (and `sm()` of a `TBATS`), as R's `sm()` of `tbats()`: the scale of the error term modelled by TBATS on the transformed errors in the space of the Box-Cox transformed data, by the joint likelihood. The transformed errors are divided by the exponent of the mean of their logarithm (digamma(1/2)+log(2) for the squares of normal errors, ...), so that the default backcasting starts from the log-scale. With an occurrence model, the zeros, whose sizes are not observed, are missing values of the scale model and have only the likelihood of the occurrence. Attached to `scale_model` (R's `implant()`), it gives the model's likelihood, `point_lik()`, information criteria and the scale of each horizon in the intervals.
* A custom loss of `TBATS` is minus its log-likelihood, as in `ADAM` and R.
* `TBATS` takes the settings of the optimiser in `nlopt_kwargs`, as `ADAM` (`B`, `lb`, `ub`, `maxeval`, `maxtime`, `algorithm`, the tolerances and `print_level`; they were separate arguments), the shape of `dgnorm` as `gnorm_shape` (was `shape`), and `step_size=None` for the default step, as `ADAM`. Its `persistence_vector` takes `ADAM`'s names, as R's `$persistence`: `alpha`, `beta`, `gamma1_j[m]` and `gamma2_j[m]` for the two states of the harmonic j of the period m, `psi` for the ARMA states and `delta` for the regressors (it was keyed by the states). It gains `ADAM`'s `persistence_level_`, `persistence_trend_`, `persistence_seasonal_` (the pairs `[gamma1, gamma2]` per period, as `persistence={"seasonal": ...}` takes them), `persistence_xreg_`, `b_value` and `om_model`.
* `ADAM` (and the classes built on it) no longer takes `nlopt_initial`, `nlopt_lower` and `nlopt_upper`, which it stored and never used: the starting values and bounds of the parameters are `nlopt_kwargs={"B": ..., "lb": ..., "ub": ...}`, with the other settings of the optimiser (now documented: `maxeval`, `maxtime`), as in `CES` and `TBATS`.
* `OMG` has `data` (the series as provided, with the sizes of the demand, R's `$data`) and `n_param` (R's `$nParam`), as R's `omg()`.
* The table of `n_param` prints its columns apart: `xreg` and `occurrence` ran together.
* `ADAM` (and the classes built on it) and `TBATS` have `arma_parameters_`, R's `$arma`: the AR and MA parameters by name, estimated or provided, as `{"ar": {...}, "ma": {...}}` with the parts the model has, or None without ARMA. The docstring of `ADAM` promised it, as it did `initial_states_`, which is `initial_value` and is no longer listed.
* A tutorial page for `TBATS` (`docs/tbats.rst`), with the examples of R's vignette on the same double seasonal series generated by `sim_gum`.
* `TBATS` takes `persistence`, `phi`, `arma` and `initial` values, as `ADAM` and R's `tbats()`, and estimates the rest: dicts fix their parts (the seasonal entries have one per period: the pair of smoothing parameters, and the sine and then the cosine coefficients of the harmonics of the initial seasonality, in the space of the transformed data), vectors need a structure that is not selected, `phi` needs a provided damped trend (otherwise they are estimated with a warning), and `arma` switches off the selection of the orders. The refits keep them, `n_param` counts them as provided, and `initial_type` is `"provided"` when some initial states are. On the same data the fits are R's (`tests/test_tbats_r_parity.py`).
* `TBATS(distribution="auto")`, the default, chooses the distribution of the error term as R's `tbats()`: the structure is selected with `"dgnorm"`, and the named distribution closest to its estimated shape on the log scale (`"ds"` below 0.71, `"dlaplace"` below 1.41, `"dnorm"` otherwise) is then fitted on that structure from the default start and from the `"dgnorm"` estimates, keeping the higher likelihood. `distribution_` is the chosen one, and its information criterion is added to `ICs`. With a loss other than the likelihood, `"auto"` is the distribution of the loss, as in `ADAM`.
* The `"dgnorm"` scale of `scaler()` (`TBATS` and `ADAM`) takes libm's `pow()`, as R's `adam_scaler()`, instead of NumPy's power, which differs in the last bit on ~5% of the inputs.
* The least squares of TBATS's global model go through the Householder QR shared with R (`src/headers/olsCore.h`, BLAS-free), so the two agree to the last bit.
* `TBATS(occurrence=...)` models an intermittent demand, as R's `tbats(occurrence=)`: a fitted `OM` / `OMG`, the provided probabilities (or 0/1) of the in-sample observations and possibly of the horizon, or an occurrence type (`"fixed"`, `"auto"`, `"odds-ratio"`, `"inverse-odds-ratio"`, `"direct"`, `"general"`) that fits `OM(model="ZXN", lags=[1])`, a level-only occurrence with the trend selected. The sizes are modelled on the non-zero observations (the Box-Cox transform, its Jacobian, the global model, the harmonics, the ARMA screen and the regressors use them only; the states evolve through the zeros), and the log-likelihood and the number of parameters include those of the occurrence. The fitted values and the skeleton and mean forecasts are the probability times those of the sizes, the median and the bounds are the quantiles of the mixture of no demand and the sizes, and `reforecast()` and `simulate()` draw the occurrence. `"none"`, the default, fits the zeros as values with a warning. `predict()` takes `occurrence` with the probabilities of the horizon. On the same data the fit, the forecasts and the bounds are R's (`tests/test_tbats_r_parity.py`).
* The admissible bounds of `TBATS` take the eigenvalues of the discount matrix with two states per harmonic, as R (the lag-expanded form only adds zero eigenvalues), from the LAPACK-free routine shared with R (`eigenModuliCore` in `src/headers/eigenCalc.h`, `_eigenCalc.eigen_moduli`): the optima often lie on the boundary of the admissible region, where NumPy's LAPACK and R's can disagree in the last bit on which parameters are admissible.
* `TBATS` warm starts its fits as R: the trend candidates from the model without trend (no trend smoothing), the ARMA from the best model (zero coefficients), the selected regressors from the best model, each kept when its loss beats the default start.
* `TBATS` fits with the transition as a sparse product (the `sparseTransition` field of the shared C++ core), as R: about 1.5 times faster on half-hourly data, the same values. The other models keep the dense product.
* `TBATS.reapply` refits all the draws in one call of the C++ refitter (`adamCore.reapply`), as R; the refitter takes one column of data per draw (their Box-Cox transforms differ). `FitResult` exposes `profileInitial`, the profile the fit starts from (the solved one with `initial="gradient"`), which the multistep losses of `TBATS` take.
* `TBATS` has the diagnostics of `ADAM`, the same functions: `plot()` (all 16 plots, the states named as the components), `rstandard()`, `rstudent()`, `outlierdummy()`, `rmultistep()`, `multicov()` (analytical, empirical, simulated), `sigma`, `extract_scale()` / `extract_sigma()`, `error_type`, `lags_used`, `data` and `holdout_data`, on the residuals of the transformed data as R. The states plot of `ADAM` drops all the head columns of the states, as R, rather than only the first one, which misaligned the states of the seasonal models with the data by `lags_model_max - 1` observations.
* The multistep errors of the shared C++ core (`ferrors`) start from the states before their targets, as R: they were off by one horizon, which made `MSEh` with `h=1` minimised at alpha=1 with a zero loss. All the multistep losses, `rmultistep()`, the empirical `multicov()` and the semiparametric / nonparametric / empirical intervals change, with T-h+1 windows. The final fit of `ADAM` takes the profile as a copy, as R: it was a view of the head of the states, which the binding passes without copying, so the head of `states` ended with the last state instead of the initial one (and `rmultistep()` started from it).
* `OM` selects its ETS model as R's `om()`: a model with `Z`, `X`, `Y`, `F`, `P` or `S` (`"ZXN"`, `"ZZZ"`, ...) goes through ADAM's pool and branch-and-bound with OM's estimator, and `ICs` holds the criteria of the models tried. These models failed before. `occurrence="general"` resolves a wildcard on each side by that selection first, as `omg()`. The combination (`"CCN"`) is not available yet and raises `NotImplementedError`. On the same data the selected model, criteria, likelihood and forecasts are R's (`tests/test_om_select_r_parity.py`).
* The rest of the Python-side numerics on the path of the likelihood use R's arithmetic, as the OM / OMG losses already did: ADAM's LASSO / RIDGE loss is R's `sqrt(sum(x^2)/n)` rather than `np.linalg.norm`, which rounds differently, and its occurrence and multistep log-likelihoods, TBATS's Box-Cox transform, its inverse and Jacobian, and its occurrence likelihood take `log()`, `exp()` and `^` through the C library (`_log_r`, `_exp_r`, `_pow_r`: NumPy's power also differs from libm on ~5% of the inputs) and sum with R's accumulator. The densities themselves come from greybox, whose Python port still uses NumPy's kernels.
* `OM` defaults to `occurrence="auto"`, as R's `om()`: it selects the occurrence type by the information criterion (`AutoOM`, whose `fit()` returns the selected model). It defaulted to `"odds-ratio"`, so the same call gave another model than R. `AutoOM` takes `loss` and `lambda_param` and passes them to every candidate, as R's `auto.om()` passes its ellipsis to `om()`; `OM(occurrence="auto", loss=...)` dropped them.
* The OM and OMG losses and the OMG likelihood use R's arithmetic: `exp()` and `log()` through the C library (`_exp_r`, `_log_r`; NumPy's SIMD kernels differ from it in the last bit on ~5% and ~0.4% of the inputs) and R's accumulators for `sum()` and `mean()`. One ulp in the OMG probability flipped a Nelder-Mead step, and ANN/ANN stopped at alpha 0.0025941 against R's 0.0025935.
* The missing values are filled for the initialisation by the same least squares as R (`_ols.na_fill`, a polynomial of time and the harmonics of the lags), so both start from the same values; the fit skips them. A warning tells when more than half of the in-sample data is missing, as R.
* The starting values of ARIMA (Hannan-Rissanen) and the ARMA screen of `TBATS` with an occurrence model put zeros at the gaps (missing and zero values, and the differences that touch them) of the centred series, keeping the lags aligned, as R.
* `TBATS` takes the missing values (NaN) for gaps, as R: the global model is fitted to the observed values, the fit skips the gaps, the likelihood and the information criteria count the observed values, the multistep losses take the windows with all their targets observed, and the ARMA screen takes zeros at the gaps.
* `CES` (and `AutoCES`) take the missing values (NaN) for gaps, as R's `ces()`: they are filled for the initialisation only, skipped by the fit, and the losses, the scale, the likelihood and the information criteria are over the observed values (the multistep losses over the windows with all their targets observed). The residuals are NaN there and `point_lik()` zero. It failed with a log-likelihood of -1e300. The fit agrees with R (`tests/test_missing_r_parity.py`). The likelihood of the multistep losses comes from a helper shared with `ADAM` (`multistep_log_lik`).
* The likelihood of the pure multiplicative ADAM ETS (`ADAM(ets="adam")` with all the components multiplicative or none) with the log-normal distribution takes the distribution of the error after the periods without an observed size exactly, as R: log(1+e) is N(-s a/2, s k) after j-1 of them, with a = 1 + sum(c_i, i<j) and k = 1 + sum(c_i^2, i<j) (`gap_variance(power=1)` and `gap_variance()`). `scaler()` and `calculate_likelihood()` take these multipliers.
* The scale of the log-normal distribution was 2(1-sqrt(1-m)), m the mean of log^2(1+e), while the maximum of the likelihood is 2(sqrt(1+m)-1), as in R.
* The likelihood of the pure additive models with the normal distribution (`ADAM` with no multiplicative component, `ES`, `MSARIMA`, `SMA`, `CES` and `TBATS`) takes the variance of the error after the periods without an observed size, the zeros of an occurrence model and the missing values, as R: sigma^2 (1 + sum(c_i^2, i<j)) after j-1 of them, with the coefficients of `covar_anal()` (`gap_variance()` in `utils/var_covar.py`). The scale, `point_lik()`, `rstandard()`, `rstudent()`, `sigma` and the OPG covariance take each error with its own variance. Verified against R (`tests/test_missing_r_parity.py`).
* `rstandard()` of an occurrence model centred the residuals on their mean over all the observations, zeros included, while R takes the observed sizes.
* The likelihood of the models with an occurrence model (`ADAM`, `TBATS` and `sm()`) has only the probability at the zeros, as R: their sizes are not observed. The differential entropy it had there shrank the scale by the share of the non-zero observations and made the likelihood unbounded with a scale model. The scale is the maximum likelihood of the observed sizes, and is de-biased by their number less the parameters (`_nobs_observed`, the intervals and the OPG covariance).
* `sm()` of an `ADAM` with an occurrence model took the fitted values of the data (the sizes times the probabilities) for the location of the sizes, and the fitted values of the scale model were the scale times the probabilities. The zeros are now missing values of the scale model, as in R, and its residuals are missing there.
* `loglik` of `sm()` of a model with an occurrence model left out the likelihood of the occurrence, as R's. `point_lik()` of a model with a scale model attached is that of the scale model.
* `sm()` of a model with missing values failed: the gaps are skipped by the scale model too, as in R.
* `TBATS` drops the observations with a missing value (NaN) of a regressor, as R: they become gaps of `y` (with a warning) and their fitted values are NaN. Missing future values of the regressors raise one.
* `TBATS(loss=...)` takes a custom loss, a callable of `actual`, `fitted` and `B`, as R's `tbats()`: the observed values (zero where there is no demand) and their fitted values in the transformed space. The fits agree with R (`tests/test_tbats_r_parity.py`).
* `TBATS(initial="gradient")`, as R's `tbats()`: the initials of the level, the trend, the harmonics and the ARMA are solved by profiling the loss at the current parameters, through ADAM's `adam_fit_or_gradient` (exact least squares in the transformed space). The fits agree with R to the last bit (`tests/test_tbats_r_parity.py`).
* The cumulative forecasts of `TBATS` with `lambda_bc` other than 1 and with an occurrence model come from the paths simulated in the transformed space and transformed back (with the occurrence drawn), as R: the sum of the skeletons (times the probabilities), or the mean or median of the sums of the paths (`point`), with their quantiles as the bounds, in `predict()` and `reforecast()`. The cumulative skeletons are R's (`tests/test_tbats_r_parity.py`).
* In `ADAM`, the values filled in for the missing ones only seed the initial states, fitted to the in-sample values only, as R: the losses, the scale, the multistep losses (complete windows), the regressors, the accuracy (the holdout keeps its NaN), `sigma`, `aicc`, `bicc`, `rstandard()`, `rstudent()`, `outlierdummy()`, `multicov()`, the empirical and semiparametric intervals and `reapply()` (which propagated NaN) take the observed values. The fitted values at the gaps are the predictions of the model.

Bugfixes:
* `ADAM(outliers="use" | "select")` refitted with the dummies the name of the fitted model (e.g. `"ETS(MAM)"`) instead of its specification, which became ETSX(ANN); it refits the model as specified, as R. The dummies are named as in R (`outlier1`, `outlier1Lag1`, `outlier1Lead1`, ...; they were `x1`, `x2`, ...) and, with `"select"`, ordered as R's `xregExpander()` (the dummy, its lag, its lead), so the selection and the coefficients are R's.
* `ADAM` without an occurrence model counted only the non-zero values as its observations in the checks of the sample size, so a series of zeros with a single one (a dummy variable, which `TBATS` forecasts with `ADAM` when its future values are missing) fell to the naive model of one observation and failed. The zeros are values without an occurrence model, as in R: it is an ETS(ANN).
* The information criteria of the candidates of `TBATS` in `ICs` counted the missing values as observations; they count the observed values, as R (the `aicc` of the model did).
* `CES.reapply()` bounds each smoothing parameter with the others at their values, the transition and the persistence rebuilt from them (`ces_bounds()`, as R's new `cesBounds()`): it clamped them to the stable range of an element of the persistence vector, as R did, which sent the refits of the non-seasonal CES to 1e+169. With a provided `a`, the draws of b were written into the states of a.
* `initial="gradient"` with a custom loss function (`ADAM` and its wrappers, `TBATS`) backcast the initials while the model kept `initial="gradient"`: it now switches to `initial="backcasting"` explicitly, with a warning, as R.
* `ADAM(ets="adam")` without ETS components (ARIMA, regressors) left the update of the states at 1 rather than the persistence times the error, in the shared C++ (`g()`): it is the conventional model there again, as in R.
* The coefficients of the regressors of `ADAM` are named after their variables, as R's (`x`, `my.var`: R's `make.names`, also in the states and the selection), rather than `xreg1`, `xreg2`; and its constant is `drift` with ETS or differences, as R, rather than `constant` everywhere.
* `ADAM` with some persistence provided (`persistence={"alpha": 0.3}`) estimated none of the rest: beta, gamma and the deltas of `regressors="adapt"` stayed at zero. The persistence is now estimated if any of its parameters is, as R; the fits agree with R's (`tests/test_adam_xreg_r_parity.py`).
* `ADAM.reapply()` with backcasting and regressors wrote the drawn coefficients into the trend: they now start the states of the regressors. The last seasonal state of a multiplicative seasonality with a zero product of the others was set to 1 rather than R's 1/0.
* `ADAM.coefbootstrap()` supports regressors: each replicate is refitted on its rows of `y` and of the regressors of the model (the selected ones with `regressors="select"`, used as they are), with the outliers not detected again, as R. It raised an error.
* With a multiplicative error, a constant and regressors, the shared C++ measurement took the constant for a regressor (exp(constant) instead of the ratio); and `reapply()` without a seasonal lag kept the original states in the first column of a draw with the estimated initials. Both fixed in the shared headers, as in R.
* The empirical and nonparametric intervals of `ADAM` with a ratio distribution (`dgamma`, `dinvgauss`, `dlnorm`) and an additive error left the forecast out of the bounds (75.9 instead of R's 483.5), and at h=1 did not divide the errors by the fitted values.
* `ADAM.outlierdummy()` took the bounds of `dgamma` from `sigma` instead of the scale, and of `dinvgauss` from a dispersion built on `sigma`, so the mixtures flagged other observations than R; the bounds are R's, from greybox (`qgamma`, `qinvgauss` with the de-biased scale, `qs`, `qgnorm`, `qlaplace`), and the logarithms are taken for all the log distributions.
* The name of an `ADAM` with `occurrence="auto"` had no occurrence type: it takes the type of the selected model, as R (`iETS(MNN)[F]`).
* `sigma` of `ADAM` divided by all the observations, while R divides by the non-zero ones (`nobs(object, all=FALSE)`); this mattered with an occurrence model.
* `ADAM` as a pure regression fitted ALM to the filled values and misaligned its fitted values with the data when values were missing.
* The ARIMA starting values of `OM` and `OMG` took the zeros of the occurrence for missing values, which left a constant series and the starting values at zero (infeasible for `OMG` with ARIMA(1,1,1), so it never moved); only the missing values are gaps there, as R.
* The OPG covariance inverted a singular outer product of the scores (at a boundary, two parameters acting alike) to values of 1e13. It is always the eigen pseudo-inverse now, in both languages: the inverse when the matrix is well conditioned, a pooled variance otherwise. `OMG` took the OPG for the losses other than the likelihood, where R takes the Hessian; its perturbations run without bounds, as R's now do.
* The occurrence model of `ADAM` did not take the ARIMA orders, the regressors (with `X`) and the `ets` form of the demand sizes, as R's `adam()` passes them to `om()`; with `occurrence="general"` both of its sides take the model of the sizes. `occurrence="auto"` failed (`AutoOM.fit()` returns the selected model, which was discarded).
* `OMG` returned the bounds penalty of the violating side, graded by the size of the violation, while R's cost returns 1e300: the optimiser took a different path from the first steps with ARIMA (log-likelihood -79.26 against R's -79.55).
* `OM` with `occurrence="fixed"` and ARIMA orders failed in the C++ (index out of bounds): a constant probability has no ARIMA, and the orders are dropped, as R now does. The name of an `OM` with ARIMA closes with the type of the occurrence, as R (`oETS(MNN)+ARIMA(1,0,0)[O]`).
* `OM` and `OMG` took the missing values for zeros: the fit now skips their update and the losses and the likelihood are over the observed values only, as R (`occurrence="fixed"` is the rate of the observed values, `actuals` keeps the gaps).
* `ADAM` with an occurrence model and missing values counted them among the zeros in the likelihood and in the number of observations of the information criteria.
* `ADAM` with an occurrence model and `dnorm`, `dlnorm` or `dinvgauss` never left its starting point (log-likelihood -1e300): a negative differential entropy of the zeros was made infinite, while R sets it to zero.
* `ADAM.point_lik()` of the mixture models did not sum to `loglik` when the entropy of the zeros was negative; it is set to zero there, as in the estimation and in R.
* `OMG`'s sub-models wrote their fitted states into the matrices of the joint cost, so the OPG covariance, which re-evaluates it, did not reproduce the likelihood and fell back to the Hessian. Its perturbations outside the bounds are now infeasible, as in R (one-sided differences there).
* The drift of ARIMA with a multiplicative error had no effect, and backcasting negated it instead of inverting it, as in R (see the R `NEWS`); fixed in the shared `src/headers/adamGeneral.h` and `adamCore.h`. ETS(M,M,N) with a seasonally differenced ARIMA and drift returned NaNs from its starting values.
* The starting value and the bounds of the drift of ARIMA come from the series differenced as the model differences it, as in R: with seasonal differencing they came from one-step differences, so the drift stopped at a bound (AirPassengers with `dlnorm`: 1.037 instead of 1.13). The parity is in `tests/test_arima_drift_r_parity.py`.
* The OPG covariance of `ADAM` (the default of `vcov()`) recomputed the density with the estimated shape of `dgnorm` held at its estimate, so its score was zero and its variance infinite (R: 0.264 on AirPassengers ETS(A,A,N)). The shape is perturbed with the other parameters, and the OPG covariance agrees with R's for `dlaplace`, `dgnorm`, ARIMA with drift, optimal initials and `ets="adam"` (`tests/test_vcov_opg_r_parity.py`).
* The quantile levels of the prediction intervals were rounded to five decimals before the quantiles were taken (they were meant for the names of the columns only), so any level but the usual ones gave slightly different bounds from R's: the bound at the level 2*pnorm(1)-1 was 0.99998 standard deviations away instead of 1. The levels are now exact, and only the column names are rounded.
* A combination with regressors (`model="CCC"`) is named ETSX(CCC), as in R; it was ETS(CCC). Its prediction intervals now agree with R's, where R counted the regressors of each model twice (see the R `NEWS`).
* `regressors="select"` selects the regressors as R's `adam()`: each model is estimated without them, `stepwise()` runs on its errors (in the form of the distribution, with the degrees of freedom of the model), and the model is estimated again with the selected ones, starting from its parameters and the ALM initials of the regressors. It ran `stepwise()` on the raw y before any estimation, which picks the regressors that explain the level of the series rather than what the model leaves, so its selections differed from R's. This is done per model, so the selection of the ETS model (`"ZZZ"`) and the combinations (`"CCC"`) take each model with its own regressors, and `predict()` takes either the selected columns of X or all those given to `fit()`. On a series with two regressors and three of noise it now selects R's regressors, with R's parameters, likelihood and forecasts, for ETS, ARIMA, two-stage, `dgnorm` and MSE (`tests/test_adam_select_r_parity.py`).
* `initial="two-stage"` started its second stage from initials that the backcasted fit had not ended with: the refit of the first stage ran from the profile the last evaluation of the loss left, not from the filled matrices, so the level of ETS(A,A,N) started 108 trend steps away (29.87 instead of R's 30.65) and the fit stopped at slightly different parameters (1.4e-4 in the level). The refit now goes through the same function as the likelihood (`fit_at_parameters()`, R's `filler()` and `adam_fitOrGradient()`), and two-stage agrees with R.
* `ADAM.predict(h, X)` wrote the new values of the regressors into the last h rows of the model's in-sample measurement matrix, a view rather than a copy, and only the point forecasts took them: the simulated intervals were centred on the forecasts with the in-sample regressors, and the next `predict()` started from the altered model (the same call returned 238.5 instead of 293.0). The measurement matrix of the horizon is now built once, from a copy, and shared by the point forecasts and the intervals.
* `OMG` handed its state matrices to the C++ fitter without copying them, so the fitter wrote its states into the arrays the next evaluation of the loss started from. This was harmless while the fitter left the head of lag-1 models alone; once it writes the backcast head there (see below), each evaluation started from the previous one's states, and OMG with lag-1 models ended at other optima than R's (ANN/ANN on Poisson data: alphas 0 and 0 with logLik -129.77, against R's 0 and 0.0059 with -129.07). The arrays are copied now, as `OM` already did.
* `OMG` with a seasonal model A and a non-seasonal model B (`model_a="ANA", model_b="ANN", lags=[1, 7]`) failed with an Armadillo index error: the joint fit loops over B's lookup table up to A's largest lag. B's lookup table and states are now padded to it, as R's `omg()`.

## v1.1.0 (Release date: 2026-09-30)

Changes:
* The ARIMA initials of `ADAM` are the initial state of the companion form, as in `ssarima`: as many as the largest ARIMA lag, held by the last ARIMA state, and all of them enter the fit. They start from the decomposition of the ETS initials (the same `smoother`) extended backwards, the backcasting seed following the current ARMA parameters, and the reported `initial["arima"]` of a backcasted model reads the time-aligned heads of the fitted states, so `two-stage` hands the backcasted fit over exactly. Mirrors the R fix; see the R `NEWS`.
* The ARIMA bounds in `CF`, `om_cf` and `omg_cf` check that each AR factor is stationary and each MA factor invertible, as in R (see the R `NEWS`); they tested the wrong MA polynomial (`"usual"`) or each coefficient separately (`"admissible"`). The check is the shared C++ `arimaReflection` (`src/headers/arimaBounds.h`), returned by `adam_polynomialiser` as `ar_reflection` / `ma_reflection`, and one helper, `arima_bounds_penalty`, replaces the three copies of the branch. `two-stage` now passes the C++ core to the initialiser, so the constant starts scaled by the AR starting values as in R.
* `confint()` and `reapply()` bound each AR / MA parameter within its factor by stationarity / invertibility, with the shared C++ `arimaParameterBounds` (`src/headers/arimaBounds.h`, exposed as `smooth.adam_general._ols.arima_parameter_bounds`), and only when `bounds` is not `"none"`, as in R. The translation of R's `arPolinomialsBounds` (bounds stuck at +-4.82) and the eigenvalue bounds of the MA parameters are gone, as are the unused companion matrices of the polynomials in the cost functions and `other["ar_polynomial_matrix"]`.
* `AutoADAM` and `AutoMSARIMA` pass `nlopt_kwargs`, `reg_lambda`, `lambda_param`, `gnorm_shape` and `step_size` on to the candidate models, as R's `auto.adam()` passes its ellipsis to every `adam()`. They were stored and ignored, so `nlopt_kwargs={"maxeval": 1}` still ran full optimisations. `AutoMSARIMA(orders=...)` no longer warns that both `orders` and the scalar orders were supplied: the scalar defaults were passed on with the dictionary.
* `ADAM` no longer takes `loss_horizon`. It was stored and never used: the multistep losses run over `h`, as in R, which has no such argument.
* With `regressors="adapt"`, the smoothing parameters of the regressors are kept in [0, 1] under the `"usual"` bounds, as in R. `CF` checked them only when given `regressors="adapt"`, which no caller passed, and `om_cf` did not check them at all; they went negative (-0.22 on AirPassengers with monthly dummies). `CF`, `om_cf` and `omg_cf` now share one `adam_bounds_checker`, as R's cost functions do, reading `regressors` from `explanatory_checked`; `om_cf` and `omg_cf` also gain the `"admissible"` check they skipped.
* The initial values of the regressors come from `alm` with an auxiliary trend dropped afterwards, as in R's `xregInitialiser`, unless a regressor is a trend itself. Without it the trend leaked into them: monthly dummies were off by 0.01 to 0.11. On AirPassengers with monthly dummies, ETSX(MMN)+SARIMA(0,0,2)[12] with `regressors="adapt"` now reproduces R's starting values and loss (515.6469, against 512.5554 with negative smoothing parameters before).
* `lags` in the lags dictionary stays the user's lags after the architector, as R's `lags`: it was overwritten with the lags of the ETS components (kept as `lags_model`), so it was empty for a pure ARIMA and missed the ARIMA lags of ETS+ARIMA, and `lags_original` held the user's. The readers that pair lags with the ARIMA orders got the wrong ones: `lags_used` returned `[]` for a SARIMA, and the LASSO / RIDGE penalty skipped the trimming of the ARMA parameters, which it shrank to zero (R keeps phi1[12] at 0.9972). `lags_original` is gone; the two readers that index lags by component use `lags_model`.
* The regularisation parameter of LASSO / RIDGE is `lambda_param` everywhere, R's `lambda`. `ADAM` also took `reg_lambda`, which it never read, while `OM` and `OMG` took only `reg_lambda`; that name is gone. A missing `lambda_param` is zero, as in R: `ADAM` warns ("You have not provided lambda parameter. I will set it to zero."), `OM` and `OMG` do not, as R's `om()`. It was one, which silently switched to the special case of estimating the initials only, and a non-positive value was replaced by one. That special case also failed for ARIMA, where it set the estimation flags to lists.
* With a multiplicative error, the initial values of the regressors of an occurrence model are estimated on the non-zero demand, as R's `subset`, with the trend in the time index of the observations. Zeros were clamped to 1e-10 before the logarithm instead, and became outliers of -23 in the regression: -0.527 against R's 0.137 on a simulated intermittent series with `distribution="dnorm"`.
* With nothing to estimate (all the parameters provided, or LASSO / RIDGE with `lambda_param=1` and backcasting), `ADAM` evaluates the loss at the given parameters, as R's `modelDo="use"`, instead of reporting an infinite loss (788.05 in R and now in Python, `inf` before). LASSO / RIDGE with `lambda_param=1` set their parameters at the shrinkage targets in the parameters checker, as in R, which also fixes the failure of the backcasting ARIMA with it.
* The LASSO / RIDGE penalty (`trim_b_for_penalty`) finds the estimated parameters in B as the filler does, as in R: with some of them provided, or with a constant or a distribution parameter after the regressors, it shrank the wrong ones.
* Provided ARMA parameters (`arma`) are passed lag by lag, the AR of each lag then its MA, as R's parametersChecker and the order in which the shared polynomialiser reads them. They were stored as all AR then all MA, padded with zeros for the parts to estimate, so with both AR and MA provided at several lags the values landed on the wrong parameters (log AirPassengers, SARIMA(1,1,1)(1,1,1)[12]: loss -230.70 against R's -267.83), and with the MA only they were read from the zeros (-278.04 against -278.73). A wrong number of provided values now switches to estimation with a warning, as in R.
* The LASSO / RIDGE penalty of `OM` and `OMG` normalises the additive regressors by their standard deviations and skips a constant, as `ADAM` and as R, whose `om()` and `omg()` now penalise the same parameters. The scales come from the shared `lasso_denominators`.
* `OM` and `OMG` start the mixed models that R flags as dangerous (and `OM` also the direct occurrence) from no smoothing, unless `B` is provided, as R's `om()` and `omg()`, with one list (`MIXED_MODELS`): OM ETS(M,A,N) with `initial="optimal"` started from the initialiser's values and converged to a loss of 92.49 against R's 81.85.
* The second optimisation attempt of `ADAM`, `OM` and `OMG` from safer starting values now happens whenever the loss is a penalty (1e100 and more), not only 1e300, and `ADAM` selects the smoothing and ARMA parameters to reset by name, as in R.
* `two-stage` on a pure ARIMA no longer reads an ARIMA state as an ETS level initial (it failed with an `IndexError` on SARIMA).
* The AR / MA parameters of `ADAM` and `MSARIMA` start from Hannan-Rissanen estimates rather than from the sample ACF / PACF. The estimation is the shared C++ `arimaHRCore` (`src/headers/arimaInitCore.h`, exposed as `smooth.adam_general._ols.arima_hr`), so the starting values are R's bit for bit. The ARIMA constant now starts consistent with the AR starting values, as in R. With `X`, the estimates come from the residuals of the least squares regression on `X` (the shared `_ols.ols`; on the differences of both for integrated models), as in R. See the R `NEWS` for the details.
* Backcasting now filters its head, matching the R side. The zero-error stretch that backcasting produces before the sample is regulated by a new `head_length` argument on `ADAM` (and so on `ES`, `MSARIMA`, `SMA`, `AutoADAM`, `AutoMSARIMA`) and on `CES`. By default it is one full lag cycle, so the final forward pass filters over the model's own backcasts, which makes the junction between the backward and forward passes accurate for the models where the state reversal is not exact -- a damped trend, ARIMA states, regressors, CES. `head_length=0` reverts to the zero-error head; larger values are experimental. Pure ETS without a damped trend keeps the legacy path bit for bit. The kernel already implemented this (`src/headers/adamCore.h` is shared with R), but `headLength` was exposed only to Rcpp, so the Python build had silently been running with the filtering off.
* ADAM's ARIMA no longer flips the drift sign in the backcasting backward pass. ADAM carries the constant in the measurement vector under an identity transition, while `ssarima`'s companion form couples it into the state; the flip compensates the companion head walk and belongs to `ssarima` only. Measured against the exact diffuse smoother on ARIMA(0,1,0), `msarima` is exact without the flip and off by exactly twice the drift with it.
* `CES` seeds its level with R's `mean()`. R sums into an 80-bit register, divides by n, then makes a second long double pass to correct the rounding; `np.mean` is a pairwise sum in double and lands one ULP away -- 101.90728900000000579 against 101.90728899999999157 on the first four observations of a quarterly series. That ULP is enough to send BOBYQA into a different basin: CES(full) reached a log-likelihood of -132.24 where R reached -120.96. The cost surfaces themselves were already identical (worst relative difference 3.2e-16 over 40 random parameter vectors, penalty region included), so only the seed was wrong. `ces_creator` now uses `_mean_r`, as `msdecompose` already did.
* `AutoCES` refuses a seasonal candidate when the sample is shorter than one seasonal cycle, where the head of the lookup table cannot carry a full set of profile cells. Mirrors R's `auto.ces()`.
* `ADAM(model="AAdA", lags=[1])` no longer raises `StopIteration`. A seasonal model needs a seasonal lag; R warns and drops the seasonal component when the maximum lag is 1, while Python kept `season_type="A"` with zero seasonal components and then failed extracting the initials. The same guard makes `model="CCC", lags=[1]` report `ETS(CCN)`, as R does.
* A named `persistence` dictionary accepts the Greek names. R takes `alpha` / `beta` / `gamma` / `delta` as aliases of `level` / `trend` / `seasonal` / `xreg`, and `python/CLAUDE.md` documents the Greek form, but only the component names were recognised: any other key left the vector at zero, silently, with no warning. `ADAM(model="AAdA", persistence={"alpha": 0.3, "beta": 0.1, "gamma": 0.2}, phi=0.9)` on AirPassengers returned a log-likelihood of -986.24 instead of R's -656.40. Both spellings now work, the component name winning when both are given.
* `tests/R scripts/ces_reference.R` loads the local R source with `devtools::load_all(".")` rather than `library(smooth)`, so the CES fixtures track the working tree the way `tests/_r_bridge.py` already does instead of whatever version is installed in the user's R library. The fixtures were regenerated against the current source.
* Fitting a large MSARIMA no longer prints `RuntimeWarning: overflow encountered in square`. During optimisation NLopt probes parameter vectors that make the state recursion diverge; on `MSARIMA(orders={"ar": [3, 0, 0], "i": [0, 1, 1], "ma": [1, 1, 1]}, lags=[1, 48, 336])` over `taylor` exactly one probe out of 209 reached `|e| ~ 2e165`, and squaring those errors in `scaler()` overflows to infinity. That infinity is the point: it flows into the likelihood, `CF()` turns the non-finite cost into the `1e+300` penalty, and the optimiser steers away. R computes the same thing and says nothing, because R does not warn on double overflow. `scaler()` now carries `@np.errstate(over="ignore")`, which suppresses the warning and not the value -- the fitted log-likelihood is unchanged to the last bit (-25674.087479231635 before and after), as are the residuals and the reported scale.
* `OM.simulate()` seeds the state recursion from the profile rather than from the head of `states`. An occurrence model's `states` head is the raw initial level, on the probability scale, while the recursion runs on the link scale -- 0.616502 against 3.004092 for an odds-ratio `ETS(MNM)` fit -- so every simulated latent value came out scaled by their ratio. R's `simulateADAMCore()` overwrites the head with `$profileInitial` for exactly this reason; `ADAM.simulate` now does the same through a `_simulate_state_head()` hook that `OM` overrides, leaving plain `ADAM` on its existing path where the two coincide.
* `ADAM.simulate()` warns when a supplied `randomizer` returns error values a multiplicative model cannot admit (`1 + e > 0`). Matches the warning R's `simulateADAMCore()` now issues, down to the wording; see the R `NEWS` for why the resulting `NaN` is left in place rather than papered over.
* Seasonal occurrence models no longer produce an all-`NaN` initial state. `_initialize_ets_seasonal_states_small_sample()` wrote the seasonal rows at `mat_vt[i + j - 1]`, one row short: `j` already counts the level (and trend), which is exactly where the seasonal rows start, as the decomposition branch has always had it with `mat_vt[i + j]`. The off-by-one overwrote the level with the seasonal formula and left every seasonal row untouched, so an `OM(model="MNM")` fit came back with `initial_value` and `profile` full of `NaN`. Any short seasonal series reaching that branch was affected, not only occurrence models.
* `OM` no longer reaches that branch at all. The creator picks between decomposition and the small-sample path on `obs_nonzero >= 2 * lags_model_max`, and on a binary occurrence series `obs_nonzero` counts the ones rather than the observations -- 20 of 72 on the seasonal test case, below the 24 threshold. `OM` already overrides `ot_logical` to all-`True` for the creator, mirroring R's `otLogicalInternal[] <- TRUE`; `obs_nonzero` now follows it. R's `adam_creator()` has no such guard and always decomposes, so this restores the R behaviour: `OM(model="MNM", lags=[1, 12], occurrence="odds-ratio")` now returns exactly R's level (0.616502), seasonal indices and profile, with identical coefficients and log-likelihood.
* An unrecognised `loss` is now an error rather than a silent substitution. `ADAM` and everything built on it warned (and only when `verbose=1`) before falling back to `"likelihood"`, so a typo returned a fit under a loss nobody asked for -- which is how `GTMAE` went unnoticed. It now raises `ValueError` listing the accepted values, matching R's `match.arg()`. `"custom"` joins the accepted set: `om()` / `omg()` set it as a string once the user's callable has been captured, exactly as R does.
* `CES` and `AutoCES` validate `loss` too. They had no runtime check at all, so the cost function's fall-through branch quietly fitted `MSE` while `model.loss` kept whatever it was given -- `CES(loss="LASSO")` and `CES(loss="nonsense")` both returned the MSE fit. They now accept only what R's `ces()` accepts (`likelihood`, `MSE`, `MAE`, `HAM`, `MSEh`, `TMSE`, `GTMSE`, `MSCE`, `GPL`) and raise otherwise; R rejects LASSO/RIDGE, the absolute and half-moment multistep losses, and callables here as well.
* `loss="GTMAE"` works. The valid-loss list and `LOSS_OPTIONS` both spelled it `GTAME`, so the name R uses -- and the only one `calculate_multistep_loss` implements -- was rejected as unknown and silently replaced by `likelihood`, while the misspelling passed validation and then crashed with `UnboundLocalError: cannot access local variable 'CFValue'`, since nothing downstream recognised it either. All thirteen multi-step losses now run: MSEh, MAEh, HAMh, TMSE, TMAE, THAM, GTMSE, GTMAE, GTHAM, MSCE, MACE, CHAM and GPL. Against R on ETS(AAN) over AirPassengers, coefficients and log-likelihoods are bit-identical for all nineteen losses, and the loss value is bit-identical for seventeen (LASSO and RIDGE differ by 1 ulp, from `norm(x)/sqrt(T)` against R's `sqrt(sum(x^2)/T)`).
* `sm` is exported from the package: `from smooth import sm`, then `sm(model, ...)`, mirroring R's call form. * The ADAM initial seeds now match R bit for bit. Two things drifted, and both reached the states through `msdecompose`, which takes the initial level and trend from a smoothed series:

On M1 MRC10 every information criterion matched R through three phases, then R fitted `ARIMA(0,1,1)` at 791.5571 while Python fitted `ARIMA(3,1,1)` at 801.4442 and lost to the standing `ARIMA(3,0,1) with constant` at 798.9922. Same on M3 N1784/N2480 and M1 QNB13: R differenced and used one MA term, Python under-differenced and compensated with AR terms and a constant. All four now reproduce R's model, log-likelihood and AICc exactly.
* `AutoADAM`/`AutoMSARIMA` could select a model with more parameters than the series has observations. The ARIMA search space was never capped by the sample size, so on M1 QNG13 (n=10, quarterly) `AutoMSARIMA` returned `SARIMA(0,2,2)[1](0,1,1)[4]` -- 10 parameters on 10 points -- and on M1 MND9 (n=39, monthly) `SARIMA(0,1,0)[1](0,0,3)[12]` with 40 parameters on 39 points. Those win because AICc's `n - k - 1` denominator turns negative once `k >= n - 1`: its correction term flips sign and becomes a reward for extra parameters, handing QNG13 an "AICc" of -179.57 against the sensible model's 32.09. R has always trimmed the orders before the search (`R/autoadam.R:264-321`), decrementing AR, then I, then MA at the highest lag until the largest estimable parameter count fits the sample; Python passed the user's maxima straight through, and its `arima_selector` did not even take the sample size as an argument. That single omission is the whole of the difference between the two search paths -- where the candidate sets overlap the two agree exactly. R's reduction is now mirrored in `auto_adam.py`, at the same point in the flow, and produces byte-identical maxima: QNG13 `ar=2,0 i=2,0 ma=3,0` and MND9 `ar=3,1 i=2,0 ma=3,2`, the same as R. Both series now select R's model with R's log-likelihood and AICc. The cap is inert when the sample is adequate (it fires only on `nParamMax > obs_in_sample`, so e.g. `n=66` with a maximum of 66 is left untouched). The AICc formula itself is deliberately unchanged: it is undefined for `k >= n - 1`, and guarding it there would paper over the search rather than fix it.
* The IMA(d,d) phase of ARIMA selection **clamped** the MA order to `max_ma` instead of skipping the candidate, so it fitted `IMA(d, min(d, max_ma))` -- a different model that R never considers. It now drops the combination outright, as R does, and walks the grid with the first lag varying fastest to match R's enumeration order (and therefore its tie-breaking). On `ar_order=0, i_order=[2,1], ma_order=[1,1], lags=[1,4]` both languages now fit exactly `(1,0), (0,1), (1,1)`, in that order.
* ETS model selection could not reach `MAN` (or `MAdN`, `MNA`) when the trend or seasonal letter was restricted. When the sample is too small for the full pool, both languages abandon the branch-and-bound and build an explicit pool instead; Python grouped each candidate by its **error** letter, so `MAN` was filed with `AMN`/`MMN` under `trend_type in ["Z","Y","M"]`. But `MAN` has an *additive* trend and only a multiplicative error, so `X` (additive-trend-only) never matched and the model was silently absent from the pool. R groups by the component letter (`R/adamGeneral.R:2483-2507`). The same mistake applied to `MAdN` (damped) and `MNA` (seasonal). On M1 QNG15 (n=10, quarterly), `ES(model="ZXZ", initial="optimal")` returned `ETS(MNN)` with `logLik=-25.9256` against R's `ETS(MAN)` at `-17.4730`, a 9.5% forecast difference; `ZZZ` was unaffected because `Z` matches both branches, which is why the bug only showed on the restricted specifications. All four affected M1 series (QNG13-16) now reproduce R's model, log-likelihood, AICc and forecast exactly. `MAN`/`MAdN`/`MNA` are also gated on `allow_multiplicative` now, so a series containing a zero or a negative value gets a purely additive pool -- R was adding them unconditionally and has been fixed to match.
* An unused seasonal lag changed the fit. R trims the seasonal entries out of `lags` — and out of the ARIMA order vectors aligned with them — once the ETS part is non-seasonal and no ARIMA order sits at a seasonal lag (`R/adamGeneral.R:487-493`, after which `m$lags` is `1`); Python kept the lag, with `architector.py` even asserting the opposite ("R keeps original `lags` intact"). The leftover lag then reached the ARIMA initial-state seed, whose condition `any(lags>1) and obs_in_sample > max(lags)*2` is identical in both languages but was seeing different input, so Python seeded a lag-**1** ARI state from an `msdecompose` **seasonal** component instead of the series level — 356.643, -173.667, 52.024 where R uses 3086.275. Same series, same orders, only an unused lag added: R stayed at -443.929604 while Python moved to -429.687604. The trim now runs in `parameters_checker`, mirroring R. Across 128 ARIMA configurations (4 series x 8 order sets x constant x ETS/no-ETS) bit-exact agreement with R goes from 56 to 120 of 128 under `initial="optimal"` and from 95 to 126 under `"backcasting"`, with **no** material differences left under backcasting. `ar_orders`/`i_orders`/`ma_orders` no longer carry the trailing zero for the dropped lag (`ADAM("NNN", lags=[12], ar_order=[1], ...)` now reports `[1]`, as R does, not `[1, 0]`) — `test_nonseasonal_arima_at_lag1_only` was asserting the pre-fix Python shape and has been updated. Genuinely seasonal models, whether through a seasonal ETS component or a non-zero seasonal ARIMA order, are untouched by the guard.
* The ARIMA initial-state seed was not normalised by the ARI polynomial for a pure `ARIMA(0,d,0)`, so for **odd** `d` it started with the wrong sign. `initialiser()` guarded the normalisation on `n_arma > 0`, meaning a model with no AR and no MA parameters to estimate never built the ARI polynomial and never divided by its tail; R divides unconditionally (`R/utils-adam.R:1352`). `(1-B)` has tail `-1`, so the seed was negated, while `(1-B)^2` has tail `+1` and hid the bug — which is why `ARIMA(0,2,d)` always agreed with R and `ARIMA(0,1,0)` did not. The polynomial is built from the differencing orders alone, so it is well defined with an empty ARMA vector. Visible whenever a constant is estimated alongside, where the bad start is not recovered: `MSARIMA(orders={'ar':0,'i':1,'ma':0}, constant=True, initial='optimal')` on M3 N250 reported `loglik=-99.145` against R's `-97.224`, and `(0,3,0)+constant` `-116.283` against `-112.113`. R is demonstrably the correct one — the drift MLE for `ARIMA(0,1,0)` has the closed form `mean(diff(y))` = 111.4688, which R returns and Python did not (it returned ~149.73). Seeds now match R's exactly, and across 128 ARIMA configurations (4 series x 8 order sets x constant x ETS/no-ETS) agreement with R rises from 50 to 56 bit-exact. Only `(0,1,0)` fits move; four of them shift in the last digits (max 7.5e-06 absolute on values near 920, i.e. ~1e-8 relative) as optimiser-path noise. One case (M3 N250, `(0,1,0)` with ETS(ANN)) previously landed on a better optimum than R by accident and now matches R's value instead.
* `CES` crashed with `IndexError: Mat::col(): index out of bounds` whenever `h` was passed to a **seasonal** fit, and for every multistep loss (`MSEh`, `TMSE`, `GTMSE`, `MSCE`, `GPL`) at any seasonality — while R fitted all of them. The profile index lookup table was built from `obs_all = len(y)`, but R uses `obsAll <- length(y) + (1 - holdout)*h` (`R/utils-adam.R:107`); the fit-time forecast block and the multistep error walk (`adamCpp$ferrors`) both step `obs + h` columns into it, so on an 80-observation series with `h=18` the table was 82 columns where 98 were needed. The benchmark never saw it because it fits without `h` and forecasts afterwards. All nine losses now reproduce R's loss value on M3 N2217 (`MSEh` 611308, `TMSE` 6.15547e+06, `GTMSE` 204.68, `MSCE` 7.54765e+07, `GPL` 128.358).
* `CES`'s reported log-likelihood was `-loss_value` for every non-likelihood loss — `loss="MAE"` on M3 N2217 reported `loglik = -63.90` (minus the MAE itself) against the likelihood fit's `-468.79`, so the information criteria of any fit-only-loss CES were meaningless. Same defect as the one already fixed for `ADAM` and `OM`. It is now a concentrated likelihood in every case. For the single-step losses that is the **Normal** likelihood re-evaluated at the fitted parameters: CES is a Normal-error model throughout — its scale, its density and its prediction intervals are all Normal — so there is no loss-implied distribution to switch to, exactly as in `ES`, which pins `distribution="dnorm"` for the same reason (`ADAM` follows the loss only because it has a `distribution` argument to follow). For the multistep losses it is the predictive likelihood of the GPL paper, rescaled from `T-h` to `T`. `loss="likelihood"` is unchanged. The re-evaluation runs against a pristine snapshot of the cost-function arguments, since `ces_cf` writes through `mat_vt` and the profile tables and the final fit leaves the *fitted* head in them — without that a seasonal `loss="likelihood"` fit stopped reporting its own loss value (`-470.0079` against `-470.0920`). Matches R for all nine losses at all four seasonalities.
* The cost functions reached R's long-double accumulator for the likelihood but not for any other loss: `MSE`/`MAE`/`HAM` and every multistep loss still used a plain `np.sum`, and ADAM's multistep helper wrote `np.linalg.norm(x)**2` where R writes `sum(x^2)` — a square root squared back, rounding twice, the same rewrite defect already fixed in `scaler()`. R accumulates `sum()`, `colSums()` and `rowSums()` in a long double register, so 27-45% of the cost evaluations along a CES optimiser path landed on a different double than R's, by 1-2 ulps. Nelder-Mead ranks its simplex by comparison, so one flipped tie separates the two trajectories permanently, and on the flat seasonal CES surfaces that showed up as visibly different optima — `seasonality="partial"` on M3 N2217 gave `MSE` 7441.14 against R's 7438.40 and `GTMSE` 194.364 against 196.149. `likelihood` was the one loss that already matched R exactly, and the one loss already routed through `_sum_r`. Every reduction now goes through `_sum_r` (which gains an `axis` argument for `colSums`/`rowSums`), and CES's inline multistep block is replaced by the shared `calculate_multistep_loss`. Across 21 monthly M3 series x 4 seasonalities x 9 losses, 17 of the 21 series are now bit-exact against R on every loss; the 4 that are not differ on `likelihood` too and did so before this change — a pre-existing multi-modal-surface split where each optimiser finds a different basin. ADAM's multistep fits improve from 17/24 to 23/24 bit-exact against R.
* `CES(b=...)` accepted a value of the wrong shape for the seasonality. `seasonality="full"` needs a complex second smoothing parameter, but a real one was taken as-is and only surfaced as a `1e+300` penalty dressed up as a fit (`loglik = -1.1e+100`, a nonsense `a`), which is exactly the infeasibility signal the "never clip" rule says must reach the user. `seasonality="partial"` needs a real one, and a complex value raised a bare `TypeError: float() argument must be...` from deep in the call stack. Both are now rejected at construction with a message naming the right shape. `"none"` and `"simple"` have no second smoothing parameter at all: a `b` supplied there is dropped with a warning rather than silently stored on the fitted model. Same guard added to R's `ces()`.
* `CES(a=...)` (and `b=`) raised `nlopt invalid_argument` and could not be used at all. Supplying the complex smoothing parameter together with backcast or complete initials and no regressors leaves nothing to estimate, and nlopt cannot be constructed with zero dimensions. R handles this by switching `modelDo` to `"use"` and evaluating the cost once (`R/adam-ces.R:633-636`); the Python fit now skips both optimiser stages the same way. `initial="two-stage"` was collateral damage — it seeds itself from a nested `initial="complete"` fit, which hit the same crash. Handing a model back its own fitted `a` now reproduces its log-likelihood exactly (`-468.78946558` on M3 N2217), and a provided `a` correctly drops out of the parameter count (5 -> 3).
* The optimiser's fallback restart began from the wrong point. When the first Nelder-Mead run terminates on the `1e300` infeasibility plateau, `estimator()` retries from the initialiser's parameter vector with the persistence entries zeroed — but the failed run's end point had already been written back over that vector, so the retry started somewhere else than R's, whose `nloptr` leaves `x0` untouched. Worst for ETS(M,A,·), whose seed always sits on that plateau: on the M1+M3+Tourism benchmark `ETS(MAdN)` was fitted to a materially worse optimum on 24 of the 26 short yearly series that disagreed with R, by up to 54.8 log-likelihood units, which then flipped the information-criterion selection and the forecast. With the retry restored, `ES(initial="backcasting")`, `ES("ZZZ")` and `ES("SXS")` reproduce R's mean RMSSE across all 5315 series, and the number of differing (method, series) fits drops from 128 to 27.
* Multiplicative components on non-positive data followed neither of R's two rules. R (`R/adamGeneral.R`) amends an explicit model pool by dropping every specification with a multiplicative error, trend **or** seasonality; switches a *selection* spec (`PPP`/`FFF`/`YYY`, or anything containing `Z`) to pure additive; and merely **warns** when the user named one specific multiplicative model, still fitting it. Python instead silently rewrote the user's explicit `M` components to `A` — `ES(model="MNM")` on a series containing a zero returned `ETS(ANA)` with no indication — while its `FFF` pool filter dropped only the error letter, leaving `AAM`, `AAdM`, `ANM`, `AMA`, `AMdM` … in play. On the benchmark this made `ES("FFF")` select a multiplicative-seasonality model where R selected an additive one on 47 of the 73 series containing a zero. Both paths now mirror R.
* `allow_multiplicative` ignored the occurrence model. R permits zeroes when an occurrence part is present (only negative values rule multiplicative components out); Python treated any zero as disqualifying, so an intermittent series always collapsed to additive components. `occurrence_model` is now threaded into the check, and the duplicate computation in `parameters_checker` reads the resolved value instead of recomputing it.
* `_initialize_xreg_states` raised `TypeError: 'NoneType' object is not subscriptable` for a multiplicative-error occurrence model with regressors (e.g. `OM(model="MNN", ...).fit(y, X=X)`). Only the slot matching the model's error type is populated, and `om()` builds its matrices with the error type flipped to additive, so the other slot is empty; R's name-based assignment is a silent no-op there. The block is now zeroed and the assignment skipped, matching R.
* `nparam` did not match R's `nparam.smooth`, which returns the estimated-parameters total **including the distribution scale** — the same value R's `logLik` reports as its degrees of freedom. Python returned the count without the scale, one lower for every ETS/ARIMA model, so `rstandard()`, `rstudent()`, `confint()`, `outlierdummy()` and `reforecast()` all used a degrees-of-freedom figure one off R's. The information criteria were never affected (they read the log-likelihood's `df` directly). `nparam` now returns the parameters-table total (the IC-weighted average for a combination, so it can be fractional); `sigma()` drops the scale again for likelihood loss exactly as R's `sigma.adam` does; occurrence models keep no scale, as in R.
* `vcov()`, and everything built on it (`confint()`, `summary()`, `reapply()`, `reforecast()`), used a corrupted state seed for models with non-optimised initials. `preparator()` mirrors the prepared initial states back into the shared `_adam_created["mat_vt"]` so the initial-value extraction can report them, which leaves the *fitted* head rather than the creator seed in the matrices the covariance closures re-fit from — so `fisher_information()` and `_opg_covariance()` profiled a different likelihood surface than the one that was maximised (e.g. `ETS(AAA)` backcasting: `log_Lik_ADAM` at the fitted `B` returned −168.789 against the model's −171.003). Consequences: Hessian entries were wrong for whichever component's head had been overwritten (the seasonal head corrupts `gamma`'s curvature, the trend head `beta`'s — `FI[gamma, gamma]` came out 101.7 against R's 2371.3), and where the shift also moved `f(B)` the OPG reproduction guard tripped and fell back silently to that same wrong Hessian. `initial="optimal"` was immune, since `B` carries the initials and overwrites the head at every evaluation. Both closures now re-fit from a copy with the pristine creator seed restored. R never had this: `vcov.adam` re-runs `adam()` from scratch and R's copy-on-modify keeps the caller's `matVt` intact. `vcov` now matches R for backcasting models — OPG bit-for-bit, Hessian to ~1e-8.
* The `dnorm` scale was computed as `norm(e)/sqrt(n)` where R uses `sqrt(sum(e^2)/n)`, and the `dlnorm`/`dgamma` scales squared a `norm()` where R sums the squares directly. The rewritten forms round twice — once in the irrational `sqrt(n)`, once in the divide — and land on a different double. On top of that, R accumulates `sum()` in a long double register and rounds back to double, while NumPy's pairwise reduction stays in double. Either difference is worth about one ulp, which is invisible until two models have *mathematically identical* likelihoods: when the optimiser drives `alpha` to zero the level is constant, so ETS(MNN)'s relative errors are ETS(ANN)'s absolute errors rescaled by that constant and the two log-likelihoods coincide exactly. R saw the tie and kept the first candidate (ANN); Python saw MNN win by 1.1e-13 and took it. `scaler()` now uses R's expressions and accumulates through `_sum_r()` (`np.sum(..., dtype=np.longdouble)`, so the squaring stays in double as in R). Across M3, 304 series fit a constant level and 14 reach the ANN/MNN tie; the selected model differed from R on 8 of them and now differs on none. Point forecasts and analytic prediction intervals were unaffected (the two parameterisations describe the same distribution at `alpha = 0`), but `interval="simulated"` was not — it draws through the multiplicative error channel — and differed by ~1% of the forecast level. Where `np.longdouble` is an alias of double the accumulator degrades to the plain pairwise sum.
* A combined model (`model="CCC"` and friends) reported a single member's likelihood and information criteria as its own — `ADAM(model="CCC")` on M3 N1505 gave `loglik=-405.365`, `aic=816.730`, `aicc=817.241`, which are ETS(MNN)'s. A combination has no single likelihood, so R returns `NULL` from `logLik()` there with the hint "did you use combinations?" and its AIC/AICc/BIC/BICc come back empty. `loglik`/`aic`/`aicc`/`bic`/`bicc` now return `nan` and raise that same warning. For the same reason `distribution_` no longer reports one member's resolved distribution (`dgamma`) but the unresolved request — `"default"`, or whatever was passed explicitly — since the members span both error types.
* A combined model's average parameter count was weighted by the *filtered* IC weights (the members above 1%, renormalised) rather than the full ones, so the dropped tail — the heavily parameterised seasonal members — was silently left out and the reported complexity came out too low: `nparam` 3.3862 against R's 3.7944 on M3 N1505. Filtering stays for the combined fitted values, where it is deliberate; the parameter count now uses the full weights and reproduces R exactly, both by default and under an explicit `distribution` (3.5808442705 for `dlnorm`).
* `rstandard()` standardised by `sigma()` — the df-unbiased residual standard deviation — where R's `rstandard.adam` divides by `extractScale()`, the estimated distribution scale. `rstudent()`'s `dlnorm` branch had the same substitution, and `dt` was not grouped with `dnorm`. Standardised residuals were therefore systematically mis-scaled (e.g. `ETS(ANN)` on `AirPassengers`: factor 34.187 instead of R's 33.949). Now matches R bit-for-bit for the additive distributions.

## v1.0.7 (Release date: 2026-08-14)

Changes:
* `plot()` now honours matplotlib's `rcParams` for figure size and accepts `dpi`. The internal figure factory hardcoded `figsize=(7, 5)`, which silently overrode `plt.rcParams["figure.figsize"]` (every plot came out 700x500 px regardless of the user's settings), and `dpi` fell through to the drawing functions as an unexpected keyword. Both are now passed to `matplotlib.figure.Figure` as `None` when not given, so matplotlib resolves them from `rcParams`; an explicit `figsize=` / `dpi=` still wins. **The default figure is therefore matplotlib's `(6.4, 4.8)` rather than the previous `(7, 5)`** — pass `figsize=(7, 5)` or set the rcParam to restore the old size. The states plot (`which=12`) keeps its panel-count-dependent `(9, 2 * n)` default, since deferring to `rcParams` there would squash a tall stack of panels, but now honours an explicit `figsize`.
* `ADAM`, `ES`, `OM` and `OMG` gain a `smoother` parameter (moved from the historical internal default to a documented argument, mirroring the R side). `smoother` selects the `msdecompose` smoother that produces the initial states — the level and trend, plus the initial seasonal indices and the seasonal profiles for multiple-seasonal models. The **new default `smoother="default"`** resolves to `"ma"` (the centred moving average) when `initial="optimal"` and to `"global"` (a global model fitted to the data) for every other initialisation method; `"ma"`, `"lowess"`, `"supsmu"` and `"global"` force the respective smoother irrespective of the initialisation. Resolution happens once via `resolve_smoother()` and is shared by all four classes (`OM`/`ES` inherit `ADAM._resolve_smoother`; `OMG` threads it through the per-side `OM` scaffold). Previously the effective default was the fixed `"global"`, so optimal-initialisation fits now seed their seasonals from the classical moving-average decomposition by default.
* `initial="gradient"` now solves the **xreg (regression) initial coefficients** for additive-error `ADAM` models, instead of falling back to backcasting (shared-C++ behaviour, so R and Python stay bit-identical). The coefficients are additional affine directions in the initial-state design, so the one-pass least-squares solve recovers them jointly with the ETS/ARIMA initials, matching `initial="optimal"` to numerical precision. Both `regressors="use"` (static) and `regressors="adapt"` (only the initial coefficient solved; the adaptive persistence stays in the optimiser) are covered. Fixes the Python (and R) dispatcher never passing the regressor count into the gradient solve, so the xreg cells were dropped from the design — threaded `xreg_number` through the four `adam_fit_or_gradient` call sites (`CF`, `log_Lik_ADAM`, preparator, estimator). Multiplicative-error xreg / ARIMA still fall back to backcasting. Degrees of freedom unchanged.
* `ADAM`, `OM` and `OMG` `vcov()` (and `confint()` / `summary()`) gain a `type` argument — `"opg"` (**the new default**), `"hessian"` or `"bootstrap"` — mirroring the R side. `type="opg"` returns the OPG/BHHH covariance `J = sum_t s_t s_t'` built from the per-observation scores (`point_lik`), positive-semi-definite by construction and finite at boundary estimates where the observed Hessian is indefinite. The score at a perturbed parameter vector is obtained by re-fitting through the cost function with a new `return_fitted` flag (`CF` for `ADAM`, `om_cf` for `OM`, `omg_cf` already had one) and forming the density (`ADAM`: concentrated distribution likelihood; `OM`/`OMG`: coupled Bernoulli). A reproduction guard falls back to the Hessian if the re-fit cannot reproduce the fitted log-likelihood, and central differences drop to one-sided at a bound. Verified against R `vcov(type="opg")` on shared data: `OM` max abs Δ ~7e-17, `ADAM`/`OMG` ~1e-5 (finite-difference noise). Non-likelihood losses and intermittent `ADAM` fall back to the Hessian, as in R. The deprecated `bootstrap=True` maps to `type="bootstrap"` with a `DeprecationWarning`.
* `ADAM`, `OM` and `OMG` gain a `point_lik()` method returning the per-observation log-likelihood, so `sum(point_lik())` equals `loglik`. `ADAM.point_lik()` mirrors R's `pointLik.adam` — the log-density of each observation under the fitted distribution/scale (plus the occurrence Bernoulli + zero-observation differential-entropy terms for intermittent models); `OM`/`OMG` mirror `pointLik.om` (Bernoulli: `log(p_t)` when the event occurred, `log(1 - p_t)` otherwise, over the coupled probability for `OMG`). Verified bit-for-bit against R on shared data: `ADAM` max abs Δ ~1e-13 across dnorm/dgamma/dlaplace/dlnorm, `OM`/`OMG` ~2e-16. This is the per-observation score foundation the OPG/BHHH covariance path uses on the R side.
* `initial="gradient"` for `OM`/`OMG` with ARIMA or xreg is now much faster (shared-C++ change, so R and Python stay bit-identical). The occurrence Gauss-Newton solve uses an exact analytic Jacobian instead of finite differences when the sub-model(s) are additive-error (`E="A"`), covering ARIMA/xreg/constant (one forward pass propagates the sensitivities instead of one pass per free initial; for `OMG` the two sides propagate jointly through the shared probability). Measured 1.4–8.3× faster (e.g. `OMG` ARIMA 5.0s → 2.8s), reaching an equal-or-slightly-better optimum, so these fits' log-likelihoods change marginally. Multiplicative-error occurrence with ARIMA/xreg keeps finite differences; pure-ETS occurrence is unchanged. The 32 live R-parity `OMG` tests still pass.
* Degrees-of-freedom accounting is now structural and identical across initialisation methods, mirroring the R side. Backcast, complete-backcast and gradient-solved initial states count towards `df` exactly as optimised ones do (the identifiable count of the initial-state design), so information criteria are comparable across `initial="optimal"/"backcasting"/"complete"/"gradient"` — verified that `ADAM` AICc matches R bit-for-bit for optimal and backcasting. For ETS the seasonal initial df uses the exact inclusion-exclusion over the seasonal periods (`df_initials_ets_level_seasonal` in `n_param.py`): single/coprime seasonality gives the usual count, non-coprime multi-seasonal is reduced, and the correction applies to `initial="optimal"` too. `OM`/`OMG` count their initials the same way (Bernoulli, no scale), `CES` counts its structural initials, `MSARIMA` counts the ARIMA initials via the shared estimator. The distribution scale is now counted for every loss (not only `likelihood`), since every reported log-likelihood is a concentrated likelihood. These change the IC of backcasting (the default) and gradient models; model selection shifts accordingly (e.g. `AutoOM` now correctly prefers a fixed occurrence when there is no occurrence dynamics).
* `ADAM(initial="gradient")` — new initialisation type ported from the R side (`R/adam-gradient.R`), a bit-for-bit parity port. Instead of the backcasting backward pass, it solves for the initial ETS state that minimises the in-sample SSE at the current persistence: an exact one-shot least-squares solve for additive models (residuals are affine in the initial state; the design is propagated analytically alongside a single forward pass) and Gauss-Newton with an analytic Jacobian (sensitivities propagated by the chain rule in one forward pass, covering both `ets="conventional"` and `ets="adam"` maths) plus a Levenberg-Marquardt fallback for multiplicative/mixed models; finite differences remain as an internal fallback and validation oracle. The entire solve runs in C++ (`adamCore.gradientSolve`, the same code the R build compiles) and is stateless, so R and Python return identical initials and fitted values to machine precision (verified across ETS(A/M, N/A/Ad/M, N/A/M) at fixed and optimised persistence: max fitted Δ ~5e-13, logLik Δ ~1e-9). Wired through `core/utils/gradient.py` (`adam_gradient_solve` / `adam_gradient_probe_basis` / `adam_fit_or_gradient`) into the cost function, estimator and preparator fit sites. Gradient counts its initial states as degrees of freedom (like `optimal`); it also covers additive ARIMA and `CES` (see the dedicated entry below), with xreg / custom-loss specifications falling back to backcasting. The B-vector excludes the ETS initials (they are solved, not optimised) and uses the backcasting-style persistence seeds, matching R.

* `OM`'s reported log-likelihood and loss value are now cleanly separated, mirroring R. `loglik` is the Bernoulli likelihood of the fitted probabilities for every loss — previously, for the fit-only losses (`MSE`/`MAE`/`HAM`) it was `-CF_value` (minus the MSE/MAE itself), which is not a likelihood, so `loglik` and the information criteria for those fits were wrong. * `OMG.nparam` counted only the jointly-optimised parameters (`len(B_joint)`), ignoring the backcast / complete / gradient initial states of the two sides — so `OMG("AAA","AAA")` reported `nparam=6` (persistence only) while its information-criteria `df` was already the correct `32`. `nparam` now returns that same df (initials of both sides included; Bernoulli, so no scale), keeping `sigma`/`confint` consistent with the IC, and the diagnostic `model_a`/`model_b` sub-models now report the same per-side df a standalone `OM` fit would (`len(B)` + that side's initials). `ADAM`/`OM` were already correct — their structural `n_param` counts backcast initials on the provided-persistence path. Now takes a writable copy (`np.array(...)`). Models whose OPG covariance is fully finite never entered this branch, which is why only the weakly-identified case failed.
* `ADAM`'s reported `logLik` for a non-likelihood loss was `-loss_value` — e.g. `-MSE ≈ -3.6` instead of the concentrated Normal likelihood `≈ -148` — so `loglik` and every information criterion for `loss="MSE"/"MAE"/"HAM"` fits were wrong and did not match R. `log_Lik_ADAM` set `general["loss_new"]`/`distribution_new` for the likelihood recomputation, but `CF` reads `general["loss"]`, so the remap was ignored and `CF` returned the raw loss. Supports R's four occurrence types: `"odds-ratio"` (`prob = a/(1+a)` from model A only), `"inverse-odds-ratio"` (`prob = 1/(1+b)` from model B only), `"direct"` (clip A's data to `[0, 1]`), `"general"` (`prob = 1/(1 + b/a)` from both). The per-error-type transform (`identity` for multiplicative-error ETS, `exp` for additive) matches R/simoes.R:131-133. Returns a `SimulateResult` with `data` holding the probability series, `model_a` / `model_b` carrying the sub-model results, `occurrence_type` set, and `log_lik = sum(log(prob))` matching R/simoes.R:138-143. Sub-models receive deterministic derived seeds (`seed` and `seed+1`) so the joint result is reproducible. The `__str__` mirrors R's `print.oes.sim` (R/methods.R:2149-2174) — header line, "Number of observations in each series:", and the true log-likelihood. A new `r_parity`-marked `test_sim_oes_r_parity.py` confirms the probability series matches R to `atol=1e-10` across all four occurrence types via the plug-in-numbers trick (a stateful `ourFunction` doles out sequential slices of a pre-generated error vector to each `sim.es` sub-call).
* `sim_ssarima(orders, lags, obs, nsim, frequency, arma, constant, initial, bounds, randomizer, probability, seed, **randomizer_kwargs)` — Python port of R's `sim.ssarima` (R/simssarima.R:83-752). Generates state-space SARIMA series via the shared C++ `adamCore::simulate` kernel. AR / I / MA polynomials are expanded via the existing `adam_polynomialiser` wrapper (`core/utils/polynomials.py`), the ARI tail fills the first column of the transition matrix `F`, and the persistence vector receives `−ARI + MA` — same companion-form layout R uses. Random AR / MA draws under `bounds="admissible"` reject coefficients whose polynomial roots fall inside the unit circle (matches R's `polyroot` check), with a `max_tries=1000` cap. `constant=False`/`True`/numeric matches R's three-way constant handling, and the model label is auto-built as `"ARIMA(p,d,q)"` / `"SARIMA(p1,d1,q1)[L1](p2,d2,q2)[L2]..."` with `" with constant"` / `" with drift"` suffixes per R/simssarima.R:716-740.
* `sim_sma(order, obs, nsim, frequency, initial, randomizer, probability, seed, **randomizer_kwargs)` — Python port of R's `sim.sma` (R/simsma.R:48-91). Thin wrapper that builds `orders={"ar": [order], "i": [0], "ma": [0]}` and `arma={"ar": [1/order]*order}` and delegates to `sim_ssarima(bounds="none")`. Strips the `arma` / `constant` fields from the result and relabels the model as `"SMA(<order>)"` (or `"iSMA(<order>)"` when `probability < 1`), matching R's post-processing block.
* Both new simulators accept the same callable-randomizer form as `sim_es`, so the same plug-in-numbers parity-test trick works — `test_sim_ssarima_r_parity.py` drives R's `sim.ssarima` and `sim.sma` via `randomizer="ourFunction"` with pre-generated errors and confirms the generated data agrees with Python to ``atol=1e-10`` (the C++ kernel is byte-identical when the inputs are).
* `sim_es(model, obs, nsim, frequency, persistence, phi, initial, initial_season, bounds, randomizer, probability, seed, **randomizer_kwargs)` — Python port of R's `sim.es` (R/simes.R:113-615). Generates ETS series from a model specification by driving the shared C++ `adamCore::simulate` kernel that R uses. The `randomizer` argument accepts either an R-style name (`"rnorm"`, `"rlnorm"`, `"rt"`, `"rlaplace"`, `"rs"`, `"rgnorm"`, `"rgamma"`, `"rinvgauss"`, `"ralaplace"`, `"rbeta"`) **or** a plain Python callable `f(n) -> ndarray` — the latter form mirrors R's `do.call(randomizer, ...)` pattern and is what powers the R-Python parity tests (`tests/test_sim_es_r_parity.py`): feed identical pre-generated errors to both sides via `randomizer="ourFunction"` in R and `randomizer=feed` in Python; the same C++ kernel then produces byte-identical output. Returns a `SimulateResult` dataclass with R-style `__repr__` / `__str__` that reproduces `print.smooth.sim` (R/methods.R:1952) line-for-line — header line, persistence vector with `alpha`/`beta`/`gamma` row labels, optional `Phi:` and occurrence line, true likelihood.
* `ADAM.simulate(nsim, seed, obs, randomizer, **randomizer_kwargs)` — Python port of R's `simulate.adam` (R/adam.R:7365-7611). Re-simulates `obs` observations from a fitted model using the same matrices the optimiser converged on. Default error distribution and scale come from the model's fitted `distribution` / `scale` (R's switch over `dnorm`/`dlaplace`/`ds`/`dgnorm`/`dlogis`/`dt`/`dalaplace`/`dlnorm`/`dinvgauss`/`dgamma`/`dllaplace`/`dls`/`dlgnorm`); a `randomizer` callable can override it. `ES`, `MSARIMA`, `SMA` inherit the method automatically. The result `model` string is suffixed with `" estimated via adam()"` so `print(sim)` reproduces R's `print.adam.sim` (R/adam.R:7613). The companion `SimulateResult` dataclass is re-exported from the top-level `smooth` namespace.
* `python/CLAUDE.md` — added an explicit carve-out under "Never add a `frequency` parameter" stating that the standalone `sim_*` simulators (and **only** them) keep R's `frequency=<int>` parameter, because they have no fitted state or input series to infer seasonality from. The rule remains in force for `ADAM`, `ES`, `OM`, etc.
* `_adam_general.adam_simulator` now passes `nComponents` to the underlying `_adamCore.adamCore` constructor (previously omitted — the constructor binding requires it, so the wrapper raised `TypeError` whenever a caller drove the C++ simulator directly). Aligns with `adam_reforecast` and the other wrappers in the same file.

ARIMA uses the existing `adam_polynomialiser()` wrapper (`core/utils/polynomials.py`) to expand each per-draw `(phi, theta)` slice into the AR / I / ARI / MA polynomials, fills `arr_f` / `mat_g` per `non_zero_ari` / `non_zero_ma`, and propagates initial ARIMA states through the polynomial transformation for `initial="optimal"`/`"two-stage"`. A new `r_parity`-marked `test_reapply_reforecast_r_parity.py` drives R from pytest and compares distributional summaries (parameter-draw means, forecast trimmed means) within Monte-Carlo tolerance.
* `ADAM.predict(interval="complete")` and `ADAM.predict(interval="confidence")` now dispatch to `ADAM.reforecast()` and return a stock `ForecastResult` — previously the two strings were accepted in the `Literal` annotation but raised at the forecaster dispatch. `"complete"` is R's alias for `reforecast(..., interval="prediction")` (joint parameter + prediction uncertainty), and `"confidence"` averages over error draws first then quantiles across parameter draws (parameter uncertainty only). Default `nsim=100` for both modes whenever the caller leaves `predict`'s `nsim` at the predict-default of 10000, matching R's `forecast.adam`.
* `ADAM.reforecast(h, interval, level, side, cumulative, nsim, ...)` — Python port of R's `reforecast.adam` (R/reapply.R:941). Internally calls `reapply()` to obtain per-draw state matrices, samples per-distribution errors via `sample_reforecast_errors` (matches R's `reforecast.adam` switch on `dnorm`/`dlaplace`/`ds`/`dgnorm`/`dlogis`/`dt`/`dalaplace`/`dlnorm`/`dinvgauss`/`dgamma`/`dllaplace`/`dls`/`dlgnorm` verbatim) and occurrence draws, runs the shared C++ `adamCore::reforecast` kernel and reduces the resulting `(h, nsim, nsim)` paths cube to a trimmed-mean point forecast plus optional `interval="prediction"` (quantile across all paths) or `interval="confidence"` (mean across error draws first, then quantile across parameter draws). `cumulative=True`, the `h<=0` short-circuit on the refitted matrix, multi-level intervals and the R-style `Lower bound (Q%)` column labels are all supported. Returns a `ReforecastResult` dataclass with `mean`, `lower`, `upper`, `level`, `interval`, `side`, `cumulative`, `h`, `paths` and `model`; a `to_forecast_result()` helper projects onto the stock `ForecastResult` shape. Phase 2 of the port — ARIMA and external regressors raise `NotImplementedError` (inherited from `reapply()`).
* `ADAM.reapply(nsim, bootstrap, heuristics, seed, **vcov_kwargs)` — Python port of R's `reapply.adam` (R/reapply.R:87). Samples `nsim` parameter vectors from a multivariate normal centred on `coef` with covariance from `vcov()` (PSD-repaired if needed), clips draws to the admissible region (ETS `usual` bounds + xreg `delta`), then re-runs the shared C++ `adamCore::reapply` kernel once per draw. Returns a `ReapplyResult` dataclass with `states (c, n+L, nsim)`, `refitted (n, nsim)`, `transition (c, c, nsim)`, `measurement (n, c, nsim)`, `persistence (c, nsim)`, `profile (c, L, nsim)`, `random_parameters (nsim, k)` plus `time_elapsed`, `y`, `fitted`, `model`, `nsim` — exactly the same set of objects R's S3 `reapply` list carries. Phase 1 of the port covers pure-ETS models with `bounds in {"usual", "none"}`; ARIMA, external regressors and `bounds="admissible"` raise `NotImplementedError` for the time being.
* Finite-difference Hessian (shared between R and Python via `src/headers/hessianCore.h`) now uses **per-parameter relative steps** `hᵢ = ε^(1/4) · max(|xᵢ|, 1)` instead of a single absolute step. The absolute step was catastrophically too small for parameters whose magnitudes matched the data scale — `initial="optimal"` / `"two-stage"` ADAM models with initial level / trend / seasonal in `B` produced FI rows dominated by floating-point noise (caught by the `all(FI==0)` retry but still imprecise). With the new rule the relative perturbation is always meaningful: `vcov()` / `confint()` / `summary()` SEs for those parameters now agree with R to better than 2% (previously 5–15% off). `initial="backcasting"` / `"complete"` ADAM, OM, and OMG are bit-equivalent to before because their parameters all satisfy `|xᵢ| ≤ 1` so `max = 1` and the step is unchanged. R's `vcov.adam` FI-refit path explicitly aliases `initialType="two-stage" → "optimal"` (R/adam.R:2728-ish) so the Hessian is computed identically for the two modes; Python doesn't need a parallel change because its FI methods don't branch on `initial_type`. Tests in `test_adam_summary_r_comparison.py` now use `rtol=2e-2` (was `rtol=0.15` on the full vcov, `rtol=0.12` on SEs); a new `aan_two_stage` scenario locks in the parity.
* Full single-step loss menu (`likelihood`, `MSE`, `MAE`, `HAM`, `LASSO`, `RIDGE`) plus custom-callable loss are now wired up for `OM` and `OMG` on both Python and R. Previously Python `OM` only honoured `likelihood`/`MSE`, Python `OMG` was restricted to `likelihood`, and R `om()`/`omg()` accepted the strings in their signatures but the cost dispatch silently routed everything except `likelihood` and `MSE` to MSE. `LASSO`/`RIDGE` reuse ADAM's `(1 − λ) · √mean(errors²) + λ · penalty(B)` formula (`R/adam.R:894-937`; Python helper `trim_b_for_penalty` factored out of `cost_functions.py` so OM/OMG/ADAM share the exact same B-trimming). The custom callable matches ADAM's `(actual, fitted, B) → scalar` API; for OMG the parameter `B` is the joint vector `concat(B_A, B_B)`. Lambda is exposed as `lambda` on the R signature and `reg_lambda` on the Python signature (consistent with ADAM's existing surface).
* `multicov()` method added to `ADAM`, `ES`, `MSARIMA`, `OM`, and `OMG`, matching R's `multicov.adam` / `multicov.smooth`. Returns the `(h, h)` covariance matrix of multi-step-ahead forecast errors as a `pandas.DataFrame` labelled `h1..hh`. Supports `type="analytical"` (closed-form via the existing `covar_anal` / `var_anal` helpers), `type="empirical"` (rolling-origin cross-product `(errorsᵀ errors) / (nobs - h)` using the existing `rmultistep` method — both R and Python call the same C++ `adamCore::ferrors` backend, so per-cell residuals are bit-equivalent), and `type="simulated"` (averages over `nsim` simulator paths via the existing `adam_simulator` pybind binding).
* `OM.sigma` / `OM.scale` now return `sqrt(mean(residuals²))` — the link-scale residual std-dev — instead of `NaN`. Matches R's `sigma.om()` (R/om.R): the OM residuals are on the link-transformed (logit / log-odds) scale, so their second moment is a meaningful scale parameter for the underlying ETS, even though there's no equivalent on the probability axis. Makes `OM.multicov(type='analytical')` produce a finite, PSD covariance matrix that agrees with R to ~5% relative tolerance under matched fits. R-side: a new `sigma.om()` S3 method is registered (R/om.R) so `sigma(om_obj)` and `multicov(om_obj)` work on the R side too — previously they returned `numeric(0)` / crashed because `sigma.adam`'s distribution switch didn't include `"plogis"`.

Bugfixes:
* Windows wheel (round two): `python/CMakeLists.txt` now resolves BLAS + LAPACK via CONFIG-mode `find_package(OpenBLAS CONFIG)` first, falling back to MODULE-mode `find_package(BLAS)` / `find_package(LAPACK)` only when no CONFIG file is present. The previous `BLA_VENDOR=OpenBLAS` hint wasn't enough — MODULE-mode FindBLAS / FindLAPACK still couldn't see vcpkg's install layout, both `BLAS_FOUND` and `LAPACK_FOUND` came back false, the `else()` branch silently compiled `ARMA_DONT_USE_BLAS` / `ARMA_DONT_USE_LAPACK` in, and `eig_gen()` (in `ar_polynomial_bounds` / `eigen_bounds`) still blew up at runtime with `eig_gen(): use of LAPACK must be enabled` on every ARIMA `reapply` / `reforecast` and on `bounds="admissible"`. vcpkg's `openblas` port ships a full `OpenBLASConfig.cmake` and is a complete LAPACK build (BUILD_WITHOUT_LAPACK=OFF), so CONFIG-mode resolves both BLAS and LAPACK in one shot via the `OpenBLAS::OpenBLAS` imported target. A new Windows-only `FATAL_ERROR` guard at the end of the BLAS/LAPACK block now fails the build immediately if LAPACK still isn't found, instead of silently shipping a broken wheel. Linux / macOS are unchanged (their system openblas / brew openblas don't ship a config file and fall through to MODULE-mode).
* `_psd_correct` (`core/adam.py`) — used by `ADAM.reapply()` to PSD-repair the vcov matrix before MVN sampling — now catches `numpy.linalg.LinAlgError: Eigenvalues did not converge` from `np.linalg.eigvalsh` and falls back to the diagonal-only matrix (PSD by construction). The convergence failure was platform-specific LAPACK iteration noise on Windows wheels, hit by `test_reapply_admissible_runs_and_returns_finite`. Same response as the existing "very-negative-eigenvalue" branch a few lines below.
* `ar_polynomial_bounds` (Python `bounds.py`) now treats `numpy.linalg.LinAlgError: Eigenvalues did not converge` from `np.linalg.eigvals` as "outside the stability region" rather than letting it propagate. The grid search starts at AR coefficient `-5.0` (R's `arPolinomialsBounds` does the same), which produces companion matrices whose eigenvalue iteration occasionally fails to converge on Windows — same matrix on Linux converges. Hiding the convergence failure was the wrong response when we had no graceful path, but now that we have one (the search just advances toward the admissible interior, exactly what `_roots_outside` is supposed to signal), it's the right call.
* `ADAM(...)` with the default `distribution=None` no longer prints a spurious `Warning: Unknown distribution: None. Switching to 'default'.` at fit time. `None` is the constructor default and means "auto-select" — it now maps to `"default"` silently in `_check_distribution_loss` (`core/checker/parameter_checks.py`). The warning still fires for genuinely-invalid distribution strings.
* `ADAM.sigma` (and the "Error standard deviation:" line in `print(m)` / `m.summary()`) now matches R's `sigma.adam` formula (R/adam.R:4625-4658): `sqrt(SS / df)` where `SS` is a distribution-specific sum of squared residuals and `df = nobs - nparam`. Previously Python returned the optimisation scale parameter (`_prepared["scale"]`) which coincides with the empirical residual std only for the Normal distribution but diverges for `dgnorm` / `dlgnorm` / `dlaplace` / `dgamma` / `dinvgauss`. `ADAM.scale` is now decoupled from `sigma` and exposes the internal optimisation scale directly (matches R's `m$scale` / `extractScale(m)`). For the AirPassengers MAM + dgnorm reference fit, `sigma` now reports `0.0366` matching R exactly (was `0.051`).
* `ADAM(distribution="dgnorm")` (and `"dlgnorm"`) now converges to the same local minimum as R for the same data. Previously Python's NLopt landed in a different basin — same cost surface, different optimiser trajectory — producing visibly different α/β/γ/shape and a misleadingly lower loss value. Root cause: Python's `initialiser.py` set the shape parameter's lower bound to `0.25` (a belt-and-braces complement to the soft penalty at `cost_functions.py:429`), while R's `adam_checkOptimizer` uses `1e-10`. NLopt's Nelder-Mead auto-generates its initial simplex from the bound box, so a tighter shape bound changed the simplex even when the boundary wasn't active at the optimum. Loosening Python's shape bound to `1e-10` (`creator/initialiser.py:877`) gives bit-equivalent fits to R for AirPassengers `MAM` + `dgnorm` (and other dgnorm/dlgnorm fits). The soft penalty `if other < 0.25: return 1e10/other` in the cost function remains as the safety net for the genuinely-pathological low-shape region.
* `print(ADAM)` and `ADAM.summary()` now show the estimated `shape` value in the distribution line for `distribution="dgnorm"` (and `"dlgnorm"` when `gnorm_shape` is available), matching R's output. Previously printed `Generalised Normal with shape=?` even after a successful fit. The shape was already estimated and stored on `self.gnorm_shape`; this fix mirrors it onto `self.other = {"shape": ...}` so the printing helper (`_format_distribution` in `utils/printing.py`) can read it.
* `ADAM(initial="optimal")` with a seasonal lag (e.g. `lags=[12]`) no longer crashes with `ValueError: could not broadcast input array from shape (12,) into shape (11,)` during the seasonal-initial filling step. The fix in `core/creator/filler.py` corrects an off-by-one in the slice that copied initial seasonal values out of the optimisation vector `B`: only `lag - 1` independent seasonals are estimated (the `lag`-th value is fixed by the sum-to-zero / product-to-one constraint), so the slice into `B` must also read `lag - 1` elements — previously read `lag`, which both clobbered the next parameter and broke broadcasting into the `mat_vt` row. The fix mirrors R's `adam_filler` (R/utils-adam.R near line 970) which uses `B[j + 2:lagsModel[…] - 1]` (i.e. `lag - 1` elements) and increments `j` by `lag - 1`, consistent with the already-correct `j += lag - 1` line in the Python code.
* `ADAM.rmultistep()` (and therefore `multicov(type='empirical')`) no longer crashes with `IndexError: Mat::cols(): indices out of bounds` on models with `i_order >= 1` or seasonal lags greater than 1 — i.e. anything where `lags_model_max >= 2` (most ARIMA models, all MSARIMA defaults, ADAM ETS with seasonal lags >= 2). Root cause: `_compute_multistep_errors` pre-sliced `index_lookup_table[:, lags_model_max:]` before passing it to the C++ `adamCore::ferrors`, which then re-applied a `+lagsModelMax` column offset internally — a double-offset that walked off the end of the table. The fix matches R's `rmultistep.adam` (R/rmultistep.R:101-111), which passes the full lookup unchanged. The case `lags_model_max == 1` (non-seasonal ETS, ARIMA(p,0,q)) used to work by luck — the off-by-one happened to stay in bounds.

Removes the `RuntimeWarning: invalid value encountered in log` that previously fired when residuals dropped below `-1`, and gives a continuous, finite scale rather than a NaN that propagates downstream.
* `coefbootstrap()` method added to `ADAM`, `ES`, `MSARIMA`, `OM`, and `OMG`, matching R's `coefbootstrap.adam` / `coefbootstrap.om` / `coefbootstrap.omg`. Returns a `BootstrapResult` (the Python analogue of R's `"bootstrap"` S3 class) with the empirical replicate matrix, mean-centred-cross-product covariance, and run metadata. `vcov(bootstrap=True, …)` and `confint(bootstrap=True, …)` now dispatch to it (previously raised `NotImplementedError`). `parallel=True` (or `parallel=<int>`) runs replicates concurrently via `joblib.Parallel` — optional dependency, install with `pip install "smooth[parallel]"` or `pip install joblib`; if not installed, a one-line warning is emitted and execution falls back to serial. The internal sampler now mirrors R's variable-length contiguous-window resampling (R/adam.R:5011-5024) so the empirical bootstrap distribution agrees with R's to within Monte Carlo noise — covered by a new `r_parity`-marked test suite spanning ADAM ETS, ADAM ARIMA, OM, and OMG. Only `method="cr"` (case resampling) is implemented; `method="dsr"` (R's `greybox::dsrboot`) is not yet ported.

Bugfixes:
* `OMG.fitted` (and `model_a.fitted`, `model_b.fitted`) now match R's `omg()` to machine precision on mixed-error scenarios (e.g. `ANA`/`MNM`). The Python OMG post-fit reconstruction was rebuilding the sub-model state-space matrices with the user-requested error/trend/season types (e.g. multiplicative for `MNM`), while R's `omgFinalFit` reuses the forced-additive matrices the joint optimiser saw. The combined link function applied to the rebuilt-multiplicative raw fits drifted from R by up to 5–10% on the probability scale. Python now mirrors R: shares the joint-optimisation matrices with each sub-model post-fit (`OMG._om_from_side`). Coefficients, log-likelihood and loss were already identical between the two implementations.
* Shared C++ numerical Hessian: `src/headers/hessianCore.h` is now the single source of truth for the finite-difference Hessian used by both R (`vcov.adam` / `vcov.om` / `vcov.omg`, plus `vcov.ces` / `vcov.gum` / `vcov.msarima` for symmetry) and Python (`_numDeriv.hessian`). The previous R-side dependency on `pracma::hessian` is removed (`pracma` dropped from `Imports`). Same algorithm, same step size, same operation order on both sides — given the same inputs, the Hessian computation contributes zero divergence between languages. The residual ADAM-with-`initial="optimal"` cross-language gap (~1e-5) is unrelated to the Hessian and traces to NLopt-vs-nloptr converging to non-bit-identical optima on multi-parameter problems with large-magnitude initial states in B; both implementations now share that floor symmetrically.
* `OM.vcov`, `OM.confint`, `OMG.vcov`, `OMG.confint` are now aligned with R's Hessian computation. Three Python-side fixes: (i) the numerical Hessian uses `bounds="none"` (matching R/adam.R:2797 `boundsFI <- "none"`) so finite-difference perturbations near a boundary parameter no longer return the 1e300 penalty and explode the inverse FI; (ii) `invert_fisher_information` takes `abs(diag(...))` to mirror R/adam.R:5226 ("just in case, take absolute values for the diagonal"); (iii) OMG sub-models now receive proper coefficient names (`alpha`, `gamma`, …) from the joint initialiser instead of falling back to `b1, b2, …`, restoring the persistence-parameter clamping in `OMG.confint`. Paired with a fix in R's `vcov.om` (R/om.R fitted-model intake) that preserves the original `initialType`/`nIterations` during the FI refit, both OM and OMG vcov / SE / CI now agree with R to machine precision (~1e-9 to 1e-17).
